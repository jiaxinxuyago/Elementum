## The card

- Construct: per pole, three rows index-aligned to `adj_chips`, each `{word, doors:{trait, scene, outside}}`. The rule (owner 2026-09-29): Every door states the trait as a conclusion the reader can repeat, then illustrates it. Imagery is illustration, never a scene to reconstruct: no clock times, no places, no narration, no detail that does not sharpen the conclusion. Cut anything the trait does not need. The three doors are three ways in to that one plain statement, and a passage is judged by whether the trait is clearer at its end, never by how complete its picture is. Trait opens on the trait claim ("Taking things in whole is how your mind works") and then illustrates it. Scene opens on a picture, one or more concrete images from the reader's ordinary life (objects, small actions) that show the trait ("One book, read twice, with notes in the margins the second time through"), and says what the picture shows. Outside opens on the reader's own act in the moment ("When the plan breaks, you …"), names the trait behind the act, and lands in its last sentence on what others see or pay ("People mistake your pace for being behind, right up until…"). The outside view is the landing, never the opening (owner 2026-09-29, Q52). Every beat is a full sentence with verbs. A picture is USED, not displayed (the sentence itself says what the picture proves), at a size the reader can own, never a single staged event. The words go to the trait, not to the set dressing. The example source (owner 2026-09-29): Draw every example from what the persona rules (its three domain words) or from ordinary life: home, money, friends, family, the body, food, rest. Work is one source among many, never the default. Keep every picture at a size the reader can recognise as their own: one venture, one city, a page a day. A figure of speech is welcome. A boast on the reader's behalf is not. (The domain words are in the cell facts. Work stays where a domain word is work: Career, Wealth, Ventures.)
- Reasoning chain: the chip → what it IS for the person (a feature, a tendency, a habit, named as such), in one plain sentence → the pictures that illustrate it (one or more, drawn from what the persona rules or from ordinary life, work one source among many and never the default, at a size the reader can recognise as their own, no time or place stamp) → the turn to the cost or the payoff → for each door, open on that door's angle (the outside door on the reader's own act in the moment, its last sentence on what others see or pay), state the trait as a conclusion the reader can repeat, keep a picture only where it sharpens that conclusion, and cut every detail that does not → write the same content three times from the three doors so any row works at any blend position.
- Style: B1–B2 zone, fragments ≤1 per passage, aphorisms ≤1–2 per assembled reading, endings vary. Every door states the trait as a conclusion the reader can repeat, then illustrates it. Imagery is illustration, never a scene to reconstruct: no clock times, no places, no narration, no detail that does not sharpen the conclusion. Cut anything the trait does not need. No clock time, no day and no named place as the anchor of an act (a cadence, "a call every Friday", is a picture, not a stamp), no filler that expands nothing ("by their second week, as onboarding"), never one acted-out event, never a claimed memory, never another person's private thoughts, never a confirmed prediction, never a safety-critical procedure as proof of character. The example is the persona's: the Horizon's picture is an opening, a venture or a father, the Steward's is savings or a steady love, the Alchemist's is learning or a quiet room, the General's is pressure or a crisis, and the outside door's last sentence hears it from the people in that domain, not from colleagues by default. The size test: if the sentence would make most readers of this chart say "that is not my life", the picture is too big. If a detail can be cut and the trait is no less clear, cut it.
- Checks: 35–55w per door, zero dashes, the sibling test, no banned register, no capacity opener on any door ("You can", "People can", "Listeners can", "Others can", "can keep you" as the first clause: the harness blocks it, owner 2026-09-30; the outside door's first sentence is the reader's own act, never what others can do), the example source (the persona's domain words or ordinary life, the read decides), each door opens on its own angle (trait: the claim, scene: a picture, outside: the reader's own act in the moment, landing on what others see or pay in its last sentence), the trait stated as a conclusion the reader can repeat in every door, no clock time and no place stamp on any door (a stated hour, "o'clock", am or pm, "at {hour} in the {morning, afternoon, evening}", "at midnight", "at noon", "it is {day} night", a named city, street, shop or season as the anchor of an act: the harness blocks on these forms), no narration, no detail that does not sharpen the conclusion (the read cuts it and asks whether the passage lost anything), the size test (a picture most readers of the chart could own, the read decides; the harness adds a non-blocking note on a number above two within four words of a venture, city, business, company, country, language, degree, startup or house).
- Exemplar (土_偏印 · Overprepared · scene): "Seventeen browser tabs, two saved courses, a notebook full of plans. The project they all point at has not moved in a month, but the researching of it has never gone better. One more book, you tell yourself, and the telling sounds exactly like last time." The exemplar passes the 2026-09-29 rule as it stands: it opens on the picture (three objects), states the trait in its second sentence (the researching moves, the project does not), and carries no stamp and no filler. Its pictures (browser tabs, saved courses, a notebook of plans) are the Alchemist's Learning, so it passes the source test and the size test as it stands.
