# Plain cost, owned cause, open door (owner 2026-10-16)

**Ruled:** "Go with all three decisions, show me rewrites first", on the research report `Operations/Business/BIZ_04_Friction_Framing_Research.md` (the audience punishes flattery faster than a sting; the reminder phrase is packaging, the reminder stance is not).

## The rule for every friction passage and friction definition

1. **Plain cost.** The cost is stated at its true size, unhedged, as an act or a loss the reader would recognise in their own week. No "can", "may", "at times" to soften (D3 and the Post-Barnum law, unchanged).
2. **Owned cause.** The reader's own need is the cause (D10, unchanged); this is where the warmth lives.
3. **Acts, not character.** The line describes what the reader does, never what kind of person the reader is; no evaluative noun for the reader's character ("harshness", "coldness") where the act can be named.
4. **Cost to others without a charge.** The cost to others names what the reader's act takes from people, never bad faith: no implied deceit or concealment ("never tell them the price"), no third parties pictured as victims of a scheme ("a race they never chose to run"), no predicted outcome ("you are the one left behind"), no verdict on the reader's worth.
5. **Three kinds of close, varied across a page:** the flat cost; the want underneath ("you never wanted the score as much as the closeness"); permission or release ("almost ready is enough"). The reminder phrase may carry only the second and third kinds, never the charge (the B9 v0.7.6 budget of three a ledger and one among the shown passages stands).
6. **Open door.** The state frame ("As a friction," "running over," "Release it.") and THE REMEDY carry the "now what"; the passages carry no advice.

## The two tests (for writers and auditors)

- **Sugarcoated** if the cost is missing or shrunk below its real size, a hedge makes it true of everyone, the flaw is renamed as a virtue, or the reader could not say whether it was true of their week.
- **Too harsh** if it judges the reader's worth or character rather than an act, predicts an outcome, pictures others as victims of bad faith, or ends a page on which every close is a loss.
