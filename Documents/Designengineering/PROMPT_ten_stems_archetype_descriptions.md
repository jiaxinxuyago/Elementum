# Ten Heavenly Stem Archetypes — Description Reference

The ten archetypes are the canonical identities of the Ten Heavenly Stems (天干 · Tiāngān). Each pairs one of the five elements with a Yang or Yin polarity. Archetype names and manifestos are **LOCKED** in DOC2 §65 and are the single source of truth for any downstream design, copy, or icon work.

Each entry below carries:

- **Stem · pinyin · archetype name · element/polarity**
- **Manifesto** (locked — quote verbatim, never paraphrase)
- **Iconic essence** — the visual object the archetype embodies (use for icon / SVG design)
- **Personality signature** — the felt character (use for content, voice, copywriting)
- **Cultural root** — the classical Chinese meaning of the stem character (use for context, never as primary signifier)

Pair discipline: each element has one **Yang** (active, expansive, raw, larger-scale) and one **Yin** (receptive, refined, contained, smaller-scale). Within a pair the two archetypes must read as the same essence in two registers.

## Wood · 甲 / 乙

### 1 · 甲 Jiǎ — The Oak · Yang Wood
> "Builds what others can only imagine. Growth is not ambition — it is the architecture."

**Iconic essence** — A structural pillar. The great trunk of an ancient tree, branches reaching upward in tiers, roots implied below. Vertical authority. The timber that frames a house.
**Personality signature** — Principled, upright, architectural. Doesn't grow toward sunlight — grows toward shape. People build their lives around an Oak without quite realizing they're doing it.
**Cultural root** — 甲 originally depicted a tortoise shell or a sprouting seed bursting its case. Classically "first" or "armored." In the stems it stands for *great timber* — wood at its most structural.

### 2 · 乙 Yǐ — The Vine · Yin Wood
> "Finds the path no one else sees. Arrives exactly where it intended."

**Iconic essence** — A climbing vine, grass, or supple tendril. Curves around obstacles, finds purchase where there shouldn't be any. Small leaves, lateral motion, never a trunk.
**Personality signature** — Adaptive, persistent, quietly strategic. Looks pliant until you notice it has reached the top of the wall. Charm is its camouflage; precision is its method.
**Cultural root** — 乙 originally depicted a bending plant shoot. Classically "second" or "secondary." In the stems it stands for *grass-and-vine wood* — the flexible, climbing form that succeeds by yielding.

## Fire · 丙 / 丁

### 3 · 丙 Bǐng — The Sun · Yang Fire
> "Doesn't choose to illuminate. Simply is light — and everything near it comes alive."

**Iconic essence** — The sun at noon. A radiant disc with rays reaching outward. A beacon, a signal fire, public blaze. Warmth that travels.
**Personality signature** — Generous, expressive, unselfconsciously bright. Doesn't perform — radiates. People warm themselves on a Sun's presence without needing permission.
**Cultural root** — 丙 originally depicted a fishing net or an elevated stand. Classically "third." In the stems it stands for *public fire* — the great illuminating flame, sun-class warmth.

### 4 · 丁 Dīng — The Candle · Yin Fire
> "Illuminates completely what it's pointed at. Nothing more. Nothing less."

**Iconic essence** — A single candle. An oil lamp. A hearth ember. Tapered flame on a small base. Light that *focuses* rather than broadcasts.
**Personality signature** — Refined, perceptive, intimate. Will not light a room — but will see the one person in it who needs to be seen. Contained, precise, attentive to the one thing.
**Cultural root** — 丁 originally depicted a nail or a standing person. Classically "fourth." In the stems it stands for *contained fire* — the lamp flame, the ember, the focused light.

## Earth · 戊 / 己

### 5 · 戊 Wù — The Mountain · Yang Earth
> "People orient their lives around it without knowing why. The ground that holds."

**Iconic essence** — A great mountain. Layered peaks, granite mass, an immovable embankment. The earth at its most monumental. A fortress wall.
**Personality signature** — Stubborn, anchored, protective. Slow to move; harder to displace. Communities form on the lee side of a Mountain because the wind breaks before it reaches them.
**Cultural root** — 戊 originally depicted an axe-like ceremonial weapon. Classically "fifth." In the stems it stands for *great earth* — mountain, dyke, immovable mass.

### 6 · 己 Jǐ — The Field · Yin Earth
> "Grows things in silence. Leaves everything it touches more alive than it found it."

**Iconic essence** — Cultivated soil. A tilled garden bed. Furrow lines and a small mound. Clay ready for shaping. Earth as worked surface, not skyline.
**Personality signature** — Nurturing, methodical, accommodating. Doesn't claim credit for the harvest — but the harvest happens only because the Field was there, season after season.
**Cultural root** — 己 originally depicted a coiled cord or thread (sometimes the self-referential "self"). Classically "sixth." In the stems it stands for *cultivated earth* — the worked soil, the productive ground.

## Metal · 庚 / 辛

### 7 · 庚 Gēng — The Blade · Yang Metal
> "Precision before intention · An edge is never given — it is forged."

**Iconic essence** — An axe head. A sword blade. Raw iron ore. Unpolished, weighted, angular. The weapon before it is even named a weapon.
**Personality signature** — Decisive, blunt, courageous. Cuts to the point before the point has finished forming. Justice-oriented; impatient with softness that isn't earned.
**Cultural root** — 庚 originally depicted a pestle or "age" marker, with overtones of renewal. Classically "seventh." In the stems it stands for *raw metal* — the unrefined blade, the weapon-grade ore.

### 8 · 辛 Xīn — The Jewel · Yin Metal
> "Perceives what is excellent the way others perceive temperature — before the question is asked."

**Iconic essence** — A jade hairpin. Polished gold. A fine carving knife. Refined metal at its most ornamental. Faceted, cut, deliberately finished.
**Personality signature** — Precise, sensitive, exacting. Notices the flaw before it has emerged; values craft as a moral category. Elegant by reflex, perfectionist by training.
**Cultural root** — 辛 originally depicted a tattooing knife used on prisoners — carrying overtones of bitterness and finely-worked pain. Classically "eighth" or "bitter." In the stems it stands for *refined metal* — polished, ornamental, precisely cut.

## Water · 壬 / 癸

### 9 · 壬 Rén — The Ocean · Yang Water
> "Holds more beneath the surface than it ever shows. Always has. Always will."

**Iconic essence** — A great river. The deep ocean. A cresting wave with current beneath. Water at scale — the kind that moves continents.
**Personality signature** — Wise, expansive, philosophical. Calm on the surface, with currents below that decide more than people realize. Patient because time is on its side.
**Cultural root** — 壬 originally depicted a person carrying a load on their back. Classically "ninth." In the stems it stands for *great water* — ocean, river, waterfall — water that flows with force.

### 10 · 癸 Guǐ — The Rain · Yin Water
> "Knows what is true before it is spoken. Nourishes what it touches without announcing it."

**Iconic essence** — A dewdrop. Morning mist. Falling rain. Vapor curling off a wet stone. Water at its smallest readable scale — the kind that gathers, then nourishes.
**Personality signature** — Gentle, intuitive, mysterious. Arrives quietly, leaves things changed; rarely takes credit. Knows things others have to be told. Receptive without being passive.
**Cultural root** — 癸 originally depicted a crossed weapon, possibly a primitive compass. Classically "tenth." In the stems it stands for *gentle water* — dew, mist, rain — water that descends rather than flows.

## Pair-mate reading guide

Use this to sanity-check that any visual or written treatment respects the Yang/Yin polarity:

| Element | Yang (larger / sharper / raw) | Yin (smaller / softer / refined) |
|---|---|---|
| Wood  | 甲 **The Oak** — structural trunk        | 乙 **The Vine** — climbing tendril |
| Fire  | 丙 **The Sun** — public radiance         | 丁 **The Candle** — focused flame |
| Earth | 戊 **The Mountain** — immovable mass     | 己 **The Field** — cultivated surface |
| Metal | 庚 **The Blade** — raw weapon           | 辛 **The Jewel** — refined ornament |
| Water | 壬 **The Ocean** — deep current         | 癸 **The Rain** — gentle descent |

When the pair is rendered side by side at any scale, the viewer should feel the *same word said in two registers* — never two unrelated things, never the same thing twice.

## Usage notes

- **Names are LOCKED** (DOC2 §65). Quote them as written: *The Oak, The Vine, The Sun, The Candle, The Mountain, The Field, The Blade, The Jewel, The Ocean, The Rain.*
- **Manifestos are LOCKED.** Quote verbatim; never paraphrase. They carry the identity-reveal weight.
- **Iconic essence** is for visual designers — the symbolic object to translate into icons, illustrations, SVGs.
- **Personality signature** is for content writers — the voice, the felt character, the behavioral signature.
- **Cultural root** is context only — never the primary signifier in user-facing copy or visual design. The product surfaces the archetype, not the etymology.

End of reference.
