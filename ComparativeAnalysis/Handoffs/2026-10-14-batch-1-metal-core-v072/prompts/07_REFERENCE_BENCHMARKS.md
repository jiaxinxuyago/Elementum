## §0 · How to use this file

Read it after the master prompt and before any descriptive field (a ledger passage, a definition, a pool desc, the nature and presence lines, a seat reading). Two products set the bar for the feeling the owner wants, which is the reader recognising a private conflict named in the words they would use for it themselves. Each excerpt below is followed by what to take from it and what this house does differently. The house exemplars (the cards' owner-ruled passages) are the target form; the benchmarks are what the target form must reach and pass. Writing that matches a benchmark's construct but keeps its hedges, its third person or its turns has not reached it.

### 1.2 What P2 does, sentence by sentence (the construct this house adopted)

1. A claim about the reader's interior, flat, in the second person: "At heart, you're extremely sensitive and adaptable."
2. The mechanism, why the drive runs: "you may have absorbed the beliefs of your culture and family." The Pattern hedges it; this house states it from the chart's supply as a fact it owns, and as the reader's own need.
3. The tension, two of the reader's own acts as an either-or: "accept certain values without question or lose yourself trying to please loved ones." The plainest words on the page; the sentence the reader screenshots.
4. The cost, as the reader's own difficulty or need, comparative: "Compared to others, you may need extra time to really know your inner self."

No picture, no object, no time of day, no place, no other person except as the object of the reader's drive. Everything is named, nothing is illustrated. The label above the passage carries the astrology ("Neptune to Moon in Taurus") so the prose carries none.

### 1.3 Take, and do differently

Take: the flat interior claim as the opener; the either-or as the centre; the cost as the reader's own need or price; the comparative frame; the concession hinge ("Whether … or not"); the reader as the subject of every sentence with the app standing beside them; no third party looking at the reader (the cost to others is the reader's own act: "lose yourself trying to please loved ones"); one tension per paragraph; pacing by the screen; the trait label as a two-word pair above the passage.

Do differently (each is a house rule): no reflex hedge on the mechanism (A10: "may have absorbed", "may need"; state the cause); no hollow affirmation or intensifier (A5: "amazing time", "total confidence", "achieve great things"); no biography as the cause (D2: "Your environment growing up has deeply affected you"); no dash (B1); no capacity opener or capacity close (A10: "you can find ways", "you can achieve"); no "not X, but Y" (B3); no participial tail (B4: "causing you to feel alive"); no phrase repeated across the screens of one reading (B6, F1); the vocabulary held to B1 to B2 (keep need, security, control, closeness, comfort, pressure, please, lose yourself, hold back, give in; drop restructuring, inherent, magnified, innate). And since 2026-10-14 the house's own additions that The Pattern does not do: the sentences progress on causal joints with few turns (B7), two or three sentences of at most two commas and two clause joints (B8, B10), the last sentence as closure (D11), tone by pole (D12), the chip's action as the spine (D13), the cost to you named outright (D3).

### 2.2 What an entry does (the construct this house takes for the unfolding)

1. The label is the chip: one to three words, a virtue on the strength side, the same drive seen from its cost on the weakness side ("Informed" and "Arrogant" are one drive).
2. The claim, flat, the trait as a fact of the type: "ISFJ personalities have a talent for noticing things."
3. The reason as a value or a need inside the person, never a circumstance: "For people with this personality type, conformity is more or less synonymous with mediocrity"; "who place great value on history and precedent." The owner's iteration 7 is this move.
4. How it shows, as a general behavioural statement, never an anecdote: "they base their conclusions on research and analysis"; "they may wait until the situation reaches a breaking point before altering course."
5. Where it leads: the strength entry closes on the effect ("This gives them the conviction …"), the weakness entry on the cost to the person or to others ("questioning the importance of love and connection"; "may boil over in a sudden, uncharacteristic outburst").
6. The weakness entry opens on the strength it grows from ("INTJs might be knowledgeable, but they're not infallible"; "ISFJs' giving, generous nature can leave them vulnerable"), which is the friction chip written as a reminder, never a criticism.
7. Across a page the eleven entries re-analyse four or five drives from new angles rather than listing eleven traits: one drive, several angles, the ledger's three-angle row.

### 2.3 Take, and do differently

Take: the label-then-unfolding shape; the unfolding in one direction (the claim, the reason, how it shows, where it leads), each sentence following from the last; the reason as the reader's own value or need; the cost side written from the strength it grows from; the same drive re-analysed from several angles; the general behavioural statement in place of the anecdote; the varied joints that carry logic ("with this mindset", "This gives them", "Rather than … they", "Further", "Moreover", "Eventually", "Even when", "In fact", "giving them", "meaning that": none repeats inside an entry).

Do differently (each a house rule): the third person becomes the second (D10: "you", never "they" or "these personalities"); the hedges go (A10: the site's "can" eleven times, "may" six, "tend to", "might", nearly all on the weakness side; the house states the cost flat); the turns thin out (B7: "But", "Unfortunately", "Although" open the site's weakness pivots; the house progresses and allows one concession); the en-dash joint becomes a comma, a "so" or a full stop (B1); the vocabulary comes down to B1 to B2 (A13: not infallible, arduous, synonymous with mediocrity, altruistic, precedent, scathingly, internalize; keep feelings, criticism, approval, credit, frustration, change); two or three sentences of at most two commas and two clause joints in place of four or five long ones (B8, B10); the close is a closure the reader keeps, not a summary (D11); the cost to you named outright, not left to "this can leave them" (D3).

## §4 · The same trait, three ways (reach, then pass)

The trait: getting ready instead of starting (this house's 土_偏印 friction chip "Never quite ready").

- **In The Pattern's construct** (the shape of P2, written here as illustration): "At heart, you're more at ease preparing than being judged on what you did. Without realizing it, you may keep learning past the point where learning helps. This may lead you to hand people your preparation instead of your answer. Compared to others, you may need extra time before you let a thing out."
- **In a 16 Personalities entry's construct** (the shape of M7 and M8, written here as illustration): "**Never quite ready.** Their respect for being well prepared means they rarely feel ready to begin. Because the next step of preparation asks nothing of them while the first attempt asks everything, they stay in preparation past the point of use. Over time this can cost them opportunities, and the people waiting on them their patience."
- **In this house's ruled form** (the card's draft exemplar, cost to you): "Getting ready is where you feel most in control. One more pass asks nothing of you, while the first try asks everything. Remember that every chance has a closing date, and most of yours close while you are still getting ready."

What the third does that the first two do not: no hedge, the reader as the subject throughout, the cause as the reader's own need, three sentences that progress without a turn, the chip's action as the action of every sentence, the cost named outright, and a last sentence the reader keeps. What it must still match: the first two's plainness, their psychological nouns and their refusal to illustrate.
