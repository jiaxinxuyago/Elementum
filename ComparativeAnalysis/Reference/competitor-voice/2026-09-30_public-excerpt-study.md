# Public-excerpt writing-style study: The Pattern, Nebula, Co-Star

Written 2026-09-30 for the owner's revisit of the door rule and the opener (the open questions in `Evaluations/2026-09-30_v06-review-samples.md` §1, §2 and §6). Extends BIZ_03 §3's voice matrix with quoted evidence; the matrix's one-line characterisations are not repeated here. Co-Star is included because it is the richest public source and one of the two anchors of REA_16 §1's voice identity (the other is The Pattern's mechanism precision).

## Method and limits

Public sources only, quoted under fair use for analysis, never reused. The hunt ran through web search (extended mode) across press (Bustle, Vice, Interview, Daily Dot, Newsweek, W, the Sunday Times via Hattie Crisell, Pedestrian, Her Campus), Substack, Medium, forums, TikTok and store listings, plus the apps' own sites. One hard limit shaped everything: the session's egress proxy blocked every page fetch (news sites, Reddit, Wikipedia, the App Store, Google Play, Substack, Medium, the apps' own domains and help centres, all of them), so each excerpt below is the text as the search index returned it, not as read on the page. Where a quote reached me only through a search summary that named several candidate pages, the row says so; where the source itself was a user's retelling rather than the app's words, the row says "paraphrased in source". Nothing is invented and nothing is smoothed. Verbatim excerpts keep their own punctuation, including em-dashes and lowercase.

Counts. Co-Star: 28 rows, of which 13 are verbatim app lines (11 notifications or Do/Don't lines, 2 store or site copy), 6 paraphrased in source, 2 parodies kept for what they exaggerate, 7 meta lines (founder, writers' room, press, user reactions). The Pattern: 19 public rows (8 verbatim reading lines, the rest store copy, founder statements and press) plus the owner's 10 transcribed screens (P1 to P10), which are the best Pattern evidence in the file. Nebula: 17 public rows, of which 8 are verbatim reading lines (6 from one web daily horoscope, 2 from a reviewer's premium content), plus the owner's 1 transcribed daily horoscope (N1, N2). The 25-to-50 target was not reachable for any app through the index alone; Co-Star came closest, Nebula is thin and its paid surfaces are unstudied.

Not reachable: Reddit (r/CoStar, r/ThePatternApp, r/astrology), the BuzzFeed and Rolling Stone screenshot galleries, YouTube transcripts, TikTok captions, the Bonds and Timing long-form pages, Co-Star's chart-profile prose, and every Nebula paid surface. The last section lists what the owner's screenshots should cover.

Excerpt prefixes: C = Co-Star (public), T = The Pattern (public), P = The Pattern (owner's screenshots, `owner-screenshots/the-pattern/2026-09-30_transcription.md`), NW = Nebula (public), N = Nebula (owner's screenshot, `owner-screenshots/nebula/2026-09-30_transcription.md`).

### 1.2 Analysis

1. Person and opener. Of the 14 verbatim notification and Do/Don't lines (C1 to C14), 7 open on an imperative (C1, C3, C7, C9, C10, C12, C13), 4 on You or Your (C4, C5, C6, C14), 1 on an abstract noun subject (C2 "nothing"), 2 are noun lists (C8, C11). Lines opening on a capacity or hedge: 0 of 14. The profile lines (C19, C20) open on trait adjectives ("bold and proud", "restless"). Co-Star's first word is a verb or the reader; it never opens on "you can" or "you may".
2. Sentence shape. 1 to 11 words (C4 has a one-word sentence, "Goodbye."; C2 is the longest at 11), median about 6. All declarative or imperative; no conditionals, no questions in the real lines (the parodies C25, C26 add a question and a 30-word threat, which is what the meme reaches for that the app does not do). Fragments are the Do/Don't format itself (C8, C11).
3. Hedging density. Across the 14 lines (about 85 words) there is no may, might, can, tend, sometimes, perhaps or often: 0 per 100 words. The only softener is "today" as a time fence (C3, C6), which limits scope without hedging the claim.
4. Structure of a claim. Prescription dominates (the imperatives, C1, C3, C7, C9, C10). The famous drag lines are trait-naming with a cause: C5 is "you do X because Y" compressed to a noun phrase ("patterns" plus "roots in your family home"). Tension-naming is absent. The cost lands alone: C3, C4, C5 carry no virtue before or after the cost. That is the Co-Star signature BIZ_03 §2 calls "named cost", and it is also the source of the backlash lines (C3, C4, C26 as the parody of them).
5. Imagery, scene and place. Co-Star names objects freely and stages nothing. The Do/Don't nouns are concrete things with no scene around them: morning mist, strong coffee, sledgehammers, sharp objects, tinfoil hats (C8, C11). C2 is a picture (being shot at without being hit). C5 names a place, "your family home", an ordinary place with no name and no time. No clock time appears anywhere; "today" is the only time word. No line narrates an event.
6. Repetition and rhythm. Nothing repeats inside a notification because a notification is one or two sentences. The only formula is the surface frame ("Your day at a glance", "Do:", "Don't:"), which sits outside the prose. Parallel structure is the Do/Don't pair itself (C8).
7. Vocabulary level and register. Grade 4 to 6 on the notifications: monosyllables and everyday nouns, no astrological term in any notification (the astrology sits in the app's "power, pressure, trouble" scaffold, not in the line). Therapy vocabulary appears as the target of a joke (C17 Codependents Anonymous, C6 "self-love" in scare quotes, C5 "unhealthy patterns"). The writers' spec (C24) confirms the intent: "Less poetry", "specific", "dark, and straightforward".
8. What makes the line shareable. The line is short enough to be a caption, it is addressed to "you" or is an order, and it says the thing the reader would not say about themselves (C5, C3). The user tweets (C27) show the mechanism BIZ_03 §2 names: the reader confesses by quoting the app. The parodies (C25, C26) show the ceiling: the meme exaggerates length and cruelty, not vagueness, which means the real lines are already read as blunt and short.

### 2.2 Analysis

The reading corpus is T1, T2, T3, T5, T6, T7, T8 and P1, P2, P8, P9, P10: 21 sentences, about 320 words. T10 is excluded from counts because its attribution is unconfirmed.

1. Person and opener. First words: an adverbial frame in 8 of 21 sentences ("At times," T2; "Today," T6; "At heart," "Without realizing it," "Compared to others," P2; "During this amazing time," P8; "If you already feel," P9; "Ideally," P1). You or Your in 6 (T1, T3, T7, P1 first sentence, P2 first sentence, P9 second sentence). It / This / These / Things in 5 (T8, P1 second, P2 fourth, P10 both). "you may" as the first words in 1 (T5). An imperative in 1 (T6, after "Today,"). The frame-first opener is The Pattern's tic: five sentences on one card (P2) open on five different frames, and the frames are what makes one sentence feel unlike the last. Sentences whose first clause carries a capacity or hedge (can, may, might): T5, P1 second and third, P2 third to fifth, P10 first: 7 of 21, about a third. None of the widely quoted lines (T1, T3, T7, P2 first two sentences) is among them.
2. Sentence shape. 7 to 26 words, median about 14. All declarative; one conditional (P9 "If you already feel secure ... these inherent traits will be even more magnified"); no fragments, no questions. Card titles (P3 to P5) are the fragments: a placement, then a two-word trait pair. On the timing surface the unit is the screen: one paragraph of 25 to 45 words per swipe, seven swipes (P8 to P10), so pacing is done by the page, not by sentence length.
3. Hedging density. About 11 hedges in 320 words (may x5, can x3, might x1, "At times", "Ideally"): roughly 3.5 per 100 words, and clustered: P2 runs three "may" sentences in a row; P1 has "can", "might", "can" in three sentences. P8 and P9 have none, and they are the sentences that sell hardest ("amazing time", "total confidence", "exactly what you want", "even more magnified"). The hedge and the intensifier live in the same reading and never in the same sentence.
4. Structure of a claim. Trait-naming with a biographical cause is the core move: P2 opens on the cause ("Your environment growing up has deeply affected you"), names the trait ("sensitive and adaptable"), then the mechanism ("absorbed the beliefs ... accept certain values without question or lose yourself trying to please loved ones"). T1 and T7 are pure trait-naming. T3 is a rule for the reader's life ("Your life works best when"). Tension-naming appears in P2's fourth sentence (accept without question, or lose yourself pleasing others) and in T1 ("Whether you're comfortable with it or not"). Prescription is rare and soft (T6, P10 "It's not a moment to be passive"). The cost lands after the virtue: P2 (adaptable, then losing yourself), T3 (adaptable, then "meant to handle a variety of experiences" as the burden in a compliment's clothes), T2 (the cost alone, framed "At times"). The timing surface has no cost at all: P8 to P10 are all virtue.
5. Imagery, scene and place. None. Across 21 sentences there is no object, no place, no time of day, no staged event. The nouns are environment, beliefs, culture, family, values, inner self, energy, opportunities, qualities, possibilities, security, experiences. The nearest thing to a picture is the marketing "mirror" (T11), the cycle name "underworld initiation" (T9) and the unconfirmed Bonds line's "traveling the same path" (T10). Time words are windows, not clocks: "Today," "during this period," "this time frame," and the dated ranges in the card titles (P6, P8). The Pattern's "how does it know" (T17) is produced entirely by naming a cause and a tension in plain words, never by a scene.
6. Repetition and rhythm. Formula phrases recur across adjacent screens: "During this amazing time" (P8), "during this period" (P9), "during this time frame" (P10), three consecutive swipes on one formula. "Your life works best/better when" appears in two accounts' cards (T3, T7). "make your life up" / "make life up as you go along" recurs across surfaces (T8, P9). Anaphora inside a card is avoided by the frame-first opener (P2's five different frames). Two mannered devices appear: the "not X, but Y" frame (P10) and the participial tail ("causing you to feel alive", P9; "making it a great time", see Nebula), both of which REA_16 lists as banned shapes.
7. Vocabulary level and register. Grade 8 to 9: Latinate abstractions (restructuring, inherent, magnified, visionary, partnerships, adaptable) in plain syntax. No astrological term inside the prose; the astrology is quarantined in the card title ("Neptune to Moon in Taurus", "Saturn transit", P2, P1) and in dated windows. Therapy vocabulary is the register itself: "your environment growing up", "absorbed the beliefs", "lose yourself trying to please", "feel secure about yourself", "inner self" (P2, P9), which is what Tatum recognised as "the exact words we were using in therapy" (T17's context). Founder statements confirm the design (Donovan avoided sign names to dodge the "new-agey" stigma and wrote in "the language of self-help"; T6, T7 are her own examples).
8. What makes "how does it know". The quoted lines (T1, T2, T3, T7, P2's first two sentences) share one shape: a flat declarative about the reader's interior with a cause attached, no hedge, no picture. The reviewer at Bustle screenshotted "At times, it can be difficult to feel a sense of security" and "deeply affected me", both cost lines. The lines nobody quotes are the hedged mechanism sentences that follow (P2 third to fifth) and the all-virtue timing pages (P8 to P10).

Reconciliation with the owner's first reads. Both reads in the transcription file hold against the public excerpts: the opener finding (biography claim, then trait pair, then a run of "may") is confirmed by Bustle quoting the same card in another account (T4); the "energy as an agent" finding (P1, P8) is the app's only nod to a material, and the public timing line (T5, "a period of significant internal restructuring") uses the same abstract-process nouns; the "no picture on any surface" finding extends to all 8 public reading lines. One addition from the public side: the frame-first opener is not a habit of one card but the house style (T2 "At times,", T6 "Today,"), and the "Your life works best when" rule-sentence is a stock card (T3, T7), so The Pattern does repeat formula sentences across users, which is the thing an individual reader never sees and a corpus reader does.

### 3.2 Analysis

The reading corpus is NW1 to NW8 and N1: 15 sentences, about 235 words. Two daily horoscopes from two dates and two signs share a template, which is the first finding: NW1 and N1 both open "Today, [Sign], the [Moon sign] moon/energy of the ... moon" and both have a third sentence "This day is ideal for ...".

1. Person and opener. First words: the astrological subject ("the Scorpio moon", "the energy of the Taurus moon") in 3 of 15; "This day" in 2; imperative in 6 (NW5, NW6, NW8, N1's last four sentences: "embrace", "Remember", "be mindful", "Stay true"); "You may / You might" in 2 (NW2, N1 second sentence); "Whether it's" and "While it's" frames in 2; "you radiate" in 1. Sentences whose first clause carries a hedge: 3 of 15 (NW1 "may stir", NW2 "You might feel", N1 "You may feel"). The hedge sits in the same slot every time: the second sentence's "You may/might feel".
2. Sentence shape. 6 to 30 words, median about 17. Declarative and imperative only; no questions, no fragments in the prose (the fragments are the store feature list, NW13). N1 is 138 words on one screen with no pacing device; NW14's "small journal entry" is accurate.
3. Hedging density. About 5 hedges in 235 words (may x2, might x1, "at times" in press only, "While it's tempting"), roughly 2 per 100 words. Low because the register does not hedge, it affirms: "ideal", "great day", "great time", "grounded and fulfilled", "clear mind" (N1, NW3, NW4).
4. Structure of a claim. Astrological cause, then a feeling the reader "may" have, then a verdict on the day, then imperatives. There is no trait-naming about the reader as a person (the sign is the person), no mechanism, no tension. The only cost is a caution inside an imperative ("While it's tempting to indulge, be mindful", N1). Virtue everywhere, cost nowhere.
5. Imagery, scene and place. Nebula is the one app of the three that illustrates, and the illustrations are stock: "cooking a delicious meal, enjoying nature, or spending time with loved ones" (N1), "simmering beneath the surface", "dive deep" (NW2). No object belongs to a person, no place is named, no time of day. The owner's first read is right that this is the door rule's real pain point in the market: a picture that could be anyone's is the weakest line on the page.
6. Repetition and rhythm. Template repetition across days ("Today, [Sign], the ... moon", "This day is ideal for", NW1, NW3, N1). Inside N1: "indulge" twice, "feel" three times, "your" eleven times. Participial tails in both horoscopes ("making it a great time", "making it a great day", NW4, N1). The "not X; instead Y" shape (NW6).
7. Vocabulary level and register. Grade 9 to 10: transformation, self-reflection, vulnerable, productive outlets, inclination, practicality, satisfaction. Astrological terms are in the prose (Scorpio moon, Taurus moon, lunar), unlike The Pattern. The register is wellness affirmation, and the rating prompt (N2, "help us enhance your predictions") shows the copy is tuned by thumbs. The premium lines (NW7, NW8) are the same register one notch vaguer, which is what the reviewer paid to find out (NW17).
8. Shareability. None observed. No public line is quoted admiringly; the quoted lines are quoted as complaints (NW7, NW8, NW17). The one positive review quotes no line (NW16). Nebula's product is the advisor chat and the birth-chart calculator (NW9, NW13); the writing is filler around them.

Reconciliation with the owner's first read. Confirmed on every point and extended by one: the template is the same across signs and dates (NW1 versus N1), so the "could be sent to anyone" judgement is not about one bad day but the format. What the owner's screenshot adds that the web lacks is the length (138 words on one screen) and the rating prompt, which explains the register.

## 4 · Comparison against Elementum's current rules

### 4a · The door rule: illustration through pictures, no clock times, no places

Which competitor lines would pass and fail the card as written ("no clock times, no places, no narration, no detail that does not sharpen the conclusion") and the gate as built (clock times, weekday stamps, seasons as anchor, street names):

| Line | Card as written | Gate as built | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| C5 "Your unhealthy patterns have roots in your family home." | fails on "no places" | passes | the market's most-cited cost line names an ordinary place |
| C11 "morning mist, strong coffee, journal / sledgehammers, sharp objects, pity party" | passes | passes | objects with no scene, the door rule's ideal |
| C2 "being shot at without being hit" | passes | passes | a picture, no place, no time |
| C3 "Don't even try to make yourself understood today." | passes | passes | "today" is a scope word, not a clock |
| N1 "cooking a delicious meal, enjoying nature, or spending time with loved ones" | passes | passes | passes the letter and is the weakest line in the file: the rule as written does not catch a stock picture |
| P2, P8 to P10, T1 to T8 (all of The Pattern) | pass trivially | pass | there is nothing to catch; The Pattern illustrates nothing |

What this says about strictness. Neither leader stages a scene, so a rule that requires a picture is already beyond the market, and "no clock times" costs nothing against the market (no competitor line has one). The strict "no places" reading is stricter than the market's best line (C5) and would have removed it; the proposed "no place stamps" (a named city, street or venue as the anchor of an act) keeps C5 and the station's kitchen and grandmother's-house lines and still removes "three ventures across two cities". The rule's real gap shows in N1: the door rule filters place and time but not genericness. Elementum already has the filter for that (the ruled domains, E6: the picture must belong to the persona), so the door card and the domain rule together are the pair that separates C11 from N1.

### 4b · The opener and the formula-phrase window

How competitors open a state line. The Pattern opens on a frame more often than on "You": "At times," "At heart," "Without realizing it," "Compared to others," "During this amazing time," "Ideally," (8 of 21 sentences). "With too much Earth, the shelter closes over the blade" is an opener of that family: a frame, then a plain statement. Co-Star opens on the verb or on "Your" and has no frames because it has no room for them. Nebula opens on the astrological cause ("Today, Taurus, the energy of the Taurus moon enhances").

Do they repeat formula phrases? Yes, and it reads as filler in the leader. The Pattern's timing reading runs "During this amazing time" / "during this period" / "during this time frame" on three consecutive screens (P8 to P10), which is the exact case the proposed window rule (a formula phrase at most once per field and never twice within three consecutive sentences across adjacent fields) would flag. Its cards also repeat the stock rule-sentence "Your life works best/better when" across accounts (T3, T7). Nebula repeats "Today, [Sign], the ... moon" and "This day is ideal for" across days (NW1, NW3, N1). The Pattern's defence inside one card is variety of frames (P2 uses five different ones), not a ban on frames. So the market's practice supports the proposed rule's shape: keep frame openers, vary them, and stop the formula word from recurring within a screen's reach.

### 4c · The capacity opener ban ("You can", "You may")

How often the leaders open on a capacity or hedge. Co-Star: 0 of 14 lines. The Pattern: about 7 of 21 sentences carry can/may/might in the first clause, but only one opens on the words themselves ("you may be entering", T5), and the sentences most quoted by reviewers and the owner's first read (T1, T2, T3, T7, P2's first two sentences) are all stated flat. The Pattern's "you can" appears once, in the tail of a summary ("Ideally, you can find ways to channel this energy", P1), the sentence a reader is least likely to quote. Nebula: 3 of 15, always in the second-sentence slot ("You may/might feel"), which is the horoscope-slop tell BIZ_03 §2 names.

Do their best-known lines open on it? No. Every line that travelled (C3, C5, C7, T1, T3, T7, P2's opener) is a declarative or an imperative. The pairs in the review file's §6 (left "You can turn pressure into a clear next step", right "When the plan breaks, you cut it to the next step") move a line from the Pattern's hedged mechanism sentences to the shape of its quoted ones. The evidence says the ban targets the market's weakest sentence type and none of its strongest.

## 5 · Observations for the door-rule and opener discussion

1. No leader stages a scene; the picture is Elementum's own move. Across 21 Pattern sentences and 14 Co-Star lines there is no object with a time and a place around it (P2, P8 to P10, T1 to T8, C1 to C14). The door rule asks for something the market does not do, which means there is no market evidence that scenes produce "how does it know"; The Pattern gets that effect from a cause plus a tension in flat declaratives (P2, T1, T2, T17). The rule is a bet, not a parity move, and the bet's cost is the one the review file already found: a stricter card than the gate.

2. The market's best-known cost line names a place, so "no places" is stricter than the market's best. C5 ("roots in your family home") fails the card as written and passes the gate. "No place stamps" keeps it, keeps the kitchen and the grandmother's house, and still removes the two cities. The evidence supports the softening in §1 of the review file.

3. Objects without a scene are already a proven shareable shape. Co-Star's Do/Don't nouns (C8, C11: tinfoil hats, strong coffee, sledgehammers, sharp objects) are pictures with no narration, no time, no place. That is the door rule's ideal outcome, and the market proves it travels. What travels is the object plus the reader's inference, not a described event.

4. The stock picture is the failure mode the door rule does not catch. N1's "cooking a delicious meal, enjoying nature, or spending time with loved ones" passes every clause of the card and is the weakest line in this file. The rule that catches it is the persona's domain (E6), not the door card; the two rules should be read together when the card is revised.

5. Hedged mechanism sentences are the lines nobody quotes. The Pattern's quoted lines are its unhedged ones (T1, T2, T3, T7, P2 first two sentences); its hedged run (P2 sentences 3 to 5, "may have absorbed", "may have caused", "may need") and its "Ideally, you can find ways" (P1) are the sentences that carry the mechanism and lose the reader. Co-Star's lines have zero hedges (C1 to C14). The capacity-opener ban and the hedge audit are aimed at the market's weakest sentence type. The §6 pairs are the right test and the right-hand column matches the shape of the market's quoted lines.

6. Formula-phrase repetition is a visible flaw in the leader, and the proposed window rule would catch it. "During this amazing time" / "during this period" / "during this time frame" on three consecutive screens (P8 to P10) is filler the reader meets one screen at a time and the writer never sees together. The rule in §2 of the review file (once per field, never twice within three sentences across adjacent fields, content-word anaphora left alone) is the right size; The Pattern's own remedy inside one card is frame variety (P2), which is what the rule leaves lawful.

7. Frame-first openers are the leader's house style, not a tic to remove. Eight of The Pattern's 21 sentences open on a frame ("At times,", "At heart,", "Compared to others,", T2, P2), and those are among its most recognisable lines. "With too much Earth," is in that family. The risk the owner named is the formula word recurring, not the frame; the market's evidence separates the two.

8. Cost placement differs by anchor and Elementum's plan sits between them. The Pattern puts the cost after the virtue (P2, T3) and its timing pages have no cost at all (P8 to P10); Co-Star puts the cost alone with no virtue (C3, C4, C5), which is what gets shared and what got the backlash. The inscription's virtue-then-cost and the door's cost-in-the-turn are the Pattern placement with Co-Star's bluntness, which is REA_16 §1's stated hybrid, now with the excerpts to show what each half looks like on the page.
