## The relationship

<!-- source-field: mechanism.base -->

**Original**

> Like answers like, the classics say: the same note struck twice rings once, and louder. When metal stands beside metal there is no reaction, only reinforcement, ore of one seam, edges off one forge. Such sameness is strength and its own hazard. Two blades in one sheath keep each other keen, or wear each other blunt.

**Rewrite**

Metal meets its own hardness in another piece of Metal. An edge laid beside an equal edge finds the same resistance, the same wish to hold its shape. Neither gives easily. Their weight can support a common task, or their contact can spend itself in scraping, with each bright surface marked by what would not yield.

## What this energy means for you

<!-- source-field: function.definition_friction -->

**Original**

> Metal is your Body, and running over, it is more physical force than your life currently spends: discipline with no outlet, stamina turning into tension you carry in your shoulders and jaw.

**Rewrite**

Metal is your Body, and running over, it is the strength you hold in reserve after the task is done. You can sit down without feeling at rest. Discipline keeps pushing, and a small interruption meets strength that has no useful place to go.

## A characteristic

<!-- source-field: ledger[0] -->

**Original**

> **Sealed-off**
>
> Letting people in stopped being something you decline and became something you cannot quite locate. Fine, you say, on the day of the diagnosis, the layoff, the breakup. The word has three hundred uses now, and not one of them means fine.

**Rewrite**

**Shuts people out**

Needing someone feels too close to handing over your judgment. You keep the difficult part to yourself, then offer a finished answer that gives other people little to work with. Help has to arrive already shaped to your standard, so even a kind offer can meet a closed hand.

## An everyday scene

<!-- source-field: ledger[1] -->

**Original**

> **Rigid**
>
> A friend was in town for one evening, the first in three years. Lights out at eleven held anyway, the way it held through the fever, the way it holds through everything. You slept on schedule and lay there knowing exactly what the schedule had cost.

**Rewrite**

**Rigid**

At ten, an invitation sits beside the schedule you wrote for the evening. You can keep every planned task and still have time for nothing that arrived unplanned. Crossing out a line feels harder than turning down the company, even when the rule has little left to protect.

## How others may experience you

<!-- source-field: ledger[2] -->

**Original**

> **Cutthroat**
>
> People collaborate with you in armor now: terms written, credit pre-assigned, nothing left ambiguous. The precautions slow everything and offend you slightly. But the stories that mandated them are specific, dated, and told to every newcomer by their second week, as onboarding.

**Rewrite**

**Cutthroat**

Your partners can end up keeping score just to protect their part of a shared task. When credit becomes a prize, even a simple exchange needs careful terms. Winning the larger share can cost the ease of working beside someone who has a stake equal to yours.

## Practical guidance

<!-- source-field: function.advise_friction -->

**Original**

> A body built for effort needs effort to be at peace. Give it a hard weekly outlet, lifting, long walks, manual work. Pair every sitting-heavy day with something physical. When patience runs short, train first and talk after.

**Rewrite**

Unspent effort can make ordinary contact feel like resistance. Give your body a short task after a long stretch of sitting. Choose a walk or something to make with your hands. Set a clear finish, then stop there. Let your hands rest.

## Energy card · Closing line

**Original**

> You bend for nothing and call it strength, and your body keeps that bargain until the day it presents the bill.

**Rewrite**

You hold your shape through effort, and keeping that same grip during rest can make recovery harder than the work.
