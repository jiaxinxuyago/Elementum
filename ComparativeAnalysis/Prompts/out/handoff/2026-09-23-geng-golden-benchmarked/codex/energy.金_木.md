## The relationship

<!-- source-field: mechanism.base -->

**Original**

> The hard prevails over the soft, the classics say, and so metal governs wood. The axe meets the trunk, the knife meets the branch, and the wild green yields shape it could not find alone. The cut is craft rather than ruin: forest becomes timber, bramble becomes orchard, under an edge that knows what to spare.

**Rewrite**

Metal gives Wood a limit. The blade takes a crooked branch at the joint, and the tree keeps the strength that would have gone into it. Growth continues around the cut. A straight piece can become a beam, a crowded crown can open to light, and the edge earns its place by leaving something useful standing.

## Carry card

<!-- source-field: carry.wide -->

**Original**

> Wood is already here in plenty, the material the knife is for.
>
> Cut what is already in front of you. Finish it.

**Rewrite**

Wood is all around you, enough to give the waiting edge a task.

Finish the next part of a project already underway.

## What this energy means for you

<!-- source-field: function.definition_catalyst -->

**Original**

> Wood is your Action, and as a catalyst, it is effort that behaves like a living thing. Whatever you plant and keep tending, money, skills, ventures, trust, grows on its own schedule and pays you back with interest, because your work compounds instead of burning off.

**Rewrite**

Wood is your Action, and as a catalyst, it is the work that leaves something usable behind. Practice gives a skill more range. Repairs keep a tool in service, and care gives a plan time to grow beyond its first good idea. Your effort has somewhere to stay.

## A characteristic

<!-- source-field: ledger[0] -->

**Original**

> **Cultivating**
>
> Improving what you already own comes easier to you than chasing what you don’t, and it shows everywhere you’ve settled. Care, in your hands, behaves like an investment: whatever you maintain appreciates, and whatever needs chasing, you mostly no longer bother to chase.

**Rewrite**

**Cultivating**

You treat ownership as a reason to keep improving something. A loose handle gets fixed before it breaks. You can take the same care with a skill that needs regular practice, even when the changes are too small to show off. Keeping something gives your effort a place to return.

## An everyday scene

<!-- source-field: ledger[1] -->

**Original**

> **Compounding**
>
> Fifty a month since college. A page a day, a call every Friday, and at no point did any of it look impressive, which is exactly why it worked. Your natural size of effort is small enough to repeat forever, and repetition is where your money and your skill actually come from.

**Rewrite**

**Compounding**

At seven, you open the same practice book for another short session. A line that took all your attention can become the place you begin next time. The pages give small efforts somewhere to collect, so each return has more behind it than a fresh start.

## How others may experience you

<!-- source-field: ledger[2] -->

**Original**

> **Wide-branching**
>
> People who chase one goal at a time find your calendar hard to explain. You keep three ventures alive across two cities, and somehow the overlaps feed each other instead of colliding. Spreading is simply how opportunity finds enough doors to knock on.

**Rewrite**

**Well connected**

People can bring you one project and find a second use for a skill you mention along the way. Your work branches through those exchanges. A contact can matter beyond the task that first brought you together, even if the connection takes time to become useful.

## Practical guidance

<!-- source-field: function.advise_catalyst -->

**Original**

> Growing things still need a gardener’s ruthlessness. Once a season, walk the whole field: feed what is fruiting, and let one dead project go without guilt. Keep the daily habits exactly as they are. And claim the growth out loud, raise the price, take the credit, sooner than feels polite.

**Rewrite**

Growth gives you more to choose from than time can hold. At the end of each week, mark the task that produced something useful. Give it a place in next week's calendar. Put one other project aside. Write down the next step.

## Energy card · Closing line

<!-- source-field: cta_verdict -->

**Original**

> You act by cutting away whatever wastes the light, and what you care for slowly ends up bearing fruit that careless hands never get.

**Rewrite**

You shape what you work on a little at a time, and steady care can give a small start lasting value.
