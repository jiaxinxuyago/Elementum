### The original: strengths and failure modes

Strengths. It is the only pile with a voice: the engraving register, the Blade's rhythm (short hard declaratives, clean stops), imagery locked to the arena, and a screenshot line on almost every field ("Clarity this constant is a climate, and the people near you dress for it", "Learning becomes the most respectable way to stand still", "Your precision leaves you as flow", "Peacetime is what the fighting was for"). Its definitions are family-level (money, skills, ventures, trust; words, cooking, the bright remark) and keep the element's chemistry in the noun (flows, compounds, heat). Its ledger scenes are the checkable kind (fifty a month, a page a day, Thursday night and Tuesday's sentence). Its classical reading is exact and unlabelled: 固执 in the Metal turn, 印旺 in the Earth definition, 成器 in the Fire base, 少阴之气 in the Water base.

### Codex blind: strengths and failure modes

Strengths. Reads the mechanism better than a zero-background writer should: the law verb *tames* rendered as the §5d gloss without having seen it ("sets a limit to the branch without ending the life that runs through it"; "makes the steel workable without deciding the edge for it"), the Earth excess as stalling not burial ("the edge stalls beside a growing heap of ore"), the thin Water state as the pair's own image ("dew holds to the steel in drops too small to fall"). Failure modes. (1) **Flattening**: pull is its weakest axis (3.50). Definitions and dot cards lose the element's noun (no flow in the Water definition, no growth in the Wood definition, no heat in the Fire definition) and the persona's key mechanism (compounding becomes "leaves something behind"; fed-before-they-notice becomes "something they can receive"). Strengths. ## 5 · Verdict

**The original is the best pile, and it is not close**: 741 to 644 to 635, forty-four field wins, a mean pull of 4.96 against 3.50 and 3.63. Its accuracy also leads (4.78), because it carries the classical mechanism in the noun (flows, compounds, heat, the seal, the forge) where Codex carries it in a paraphrase. The only axis it does not win is audience fit (3.98 against blind's 4.35), and that gap is real: a few rows sit above the zone, a few scenes assert a life, and two directives are statements. The ten fields lost were lost on exactly those edges, not on the writing.

**Between the two Codex passes, blind beats benchmarked** (644 to 635; seven wins to three; ahead on 24 fields head to head, behind on 17). The difference is small in total but different in kind:

- Blind's wins are mechanism wins: the turns and carry cuts on the two pages where the state is the whole point (Earth heavy, Water thin), a persona cost the original left implicit, and advice written for the chart's real volume. Blind produced the two highest-scoring Codex lines of the round and the cleanest carry-card rows. It failed on pull, on virtue-chips and on one band remedy.
- Benchmarked's wins are door wins: it saw how a trait row and an outside row are built and built them that way, and it produced the leanest advice. But it paid for the sight of the original in drift (an Action image on the Mind door, a networking chip on the Horizon, hedged openers everywhere) and in borrowed props that read as anchoring rather than voice. Its total is lower than blind's because the Earth turn, its carry, and the P4 chips lost more than the ledger rows gained.

**What this says about Codex as a writer for this system.** It is a competent mechanism reader and a weak voice. Blind, it reconstructed the law verbs, the excess idioms and the persona cost dimensions from the primer and the classical file alone, and its lines are honest, plain and checkable. What it cannot do from the prompt pack as it stands is the thing the product is named for: the engraving. Its sentences run long and compound where the Blade asks for short and hard; its definitions inventory the persona in generic nouns and leave the element out; its dot cards, the one field that must read alone on a card, are its worst class in both passes. Showing it the original did not fix this. The two passes share an attractor (an identical face, an identical gift phrase, near-identical definitions, the same evening with a practice file and a pot on the stove), which means the benchmark shifted what Codex copied, not how it writes. The benchmarked pass is the original's outline in Codex's sentences; the blind pass is Codex's own reading in Codex's sentences, and the second is the more useful artifact for the owner, because its errors are legible as errors of the pack rather than as echoes.

**What the prompt pack should change for the next round.**

1. **Extend the hedge ban to the capacity opener.** "You can", "People can", "Listeners can" open most Codex chips and rows. Add them to the reflex-hedge list for chip descs, ledger rows and dot cards; a shadow desc that begins "You can" has already lost its symptom.
2. **Make the chip law's negative examples Codex-shaped.** The pack says "symptom, not a bare image"; Codex failed on "symptom, not a virtue". Add: "Steady under pressure" fails, "Crisis performer" passes; "Speaks with ease" fails, "No dressed-up answers" passes.
3. **Require the element's noun in the definition and the base.** Both Codex passes wrote element-blind definitions (no flow, no growth, no heat). State it as a check: one concrete noun from the pair's chemistry in the definition's first sentence.
4. **Require the definition to be family-level by construction.** Ask for one inventory item per persona of the family (a Steward noun and a Horizon noun; an Artisan noun and a Virtuoso noun). Codex wrote Steward-only and Artisan-only definitions in both passes.
5. **State the door law for rows as a hard check.** A trait row carries no clock time; a scene row opens on one; an outside row opens on other people. Blind mixed the trait door three times; benchmarked's chips used outside words on trait rows.
6. **State the band's remedy verb beside every advice field.** Overfueled → Channel (an outlet), Underfueled → Refill (intake). Blind wrote brakes for an Overfueled Body; the card did not say which verb this page owes.
7. **Run the repetition law across the rows of one page, not only the pair's fields.** "The list gets shorter" / "The list is short" is the case; the harness saw the four-gram and let it through.
8. **Give the stem's rhythm temperament a soft check the read reports.** Codex's mean sentence is a compound clause; the Blade's row asks for short hard declaratives. The freedom clause says this is guidance, so report it as guidance, but report it: a count of sentences over twenty words per field would have named the flattening.
9. **In benchmarked mode, forbid the original's images, not only its four-grams.** The reuse check caught nothing because Codex paraphrased the props (saved course, one trusted voice, something physical, the invitation, the resting blade). Add: no image, prop or scene from the original field, and no image from any other field of the same core (the Fire turn's "keeps its first shape" landed in the Body shadow).
10. **Mark cta_verdict as pole-neutral in the card.** Both passes bound the dot card to the shown pole; the field is one per pair and must read on either pole's page.
11. **Ask for two candidates on the pull-heavy fields** (manifesto, presence, the dot card, the ledger scene) and let the read pick, since these are where Codex is weakest and where one line decides the screenshot.
12. **Keep the blind pass as the default and change what "benchmarked" means.** Instead of the whole original, hand the model one named axis to beat per field (the door, the arena noun, the cadence of the advice, the four-question fail the original carries) and withhold the sentence itself. That converts the benchmark from a template to a target, which is what this round shows Codex needs.
