## 3 · Systemic patterns

1. **Balanced pages tell a balanced reader they run short. MAJOR (W-001).** This happens on 3 of the 5 Water Balanced pages, and the advice openers show it on about 13 of the 25 corpus-wide. Examples: P171 "Your mind takes in less than it could use" under "In balance, what comes in and what goes out keep the same pace". P175 "Little Water comes in" under "You spend and refill in turn". Fire and Earth on Water pass, because their catalyst advice opens neutrally.
2. **The amount of Water stands in for the owned cause. MAJOR (W-002).** It affects 7 unique passages on 9 pages, plus 2 borderline passages that also name a need. The same move is live on Wood pages, and the Earth equivalent was fixed in round 1. Examples: "More Water moves through you than comes back in, so you drift" (P139). "Little Water backs you, and you jump in before reading the plan" (P149). Each rewrite keeps the comparative frame and puts the need in place of the amount.
3. **The 2+2 picker can empty a persona. MAJOR (W-003)**, though small in count: 3 pages corpus-wide (P135, P150, Fire P057). Examples: P135 shows no Steward. P150 is 2+1 and sets two near-opposites side by side.
4. **The shared fields reuse the ledger's words. Not MAJOR.** The advice, remedy, edge and pair definition are fixed texts, and they often repeat a ledger word on the page. Examples: "work" ×4 on P131, P132, P172 (advice); "ask / answer / question" ×3 on P124, P126, P171 (advice and edge); "pour" ×3 on P139; "someone" ×3 on P142, P143, P174. W-023, W-025, W-031 to W-033 and W-035 remove the worst of these. What is left is within tolerance.
5. **Catalyst closes end on someone else. Not MAJOR; the owner should confirm.** About 20 of the 40 unique catalyst passages close their drive or cost_self angle with another person, or the lost chance, as the subject. D11 asks for the reader as the subject. M1's "price stated flat" may license this. Examples: "Those who trust your eye get there before the crowd" (P136, drive). "Bargains and good seats often go to whoever answered while you were still weighing it up" (P137, cost_self). They read well, so I have filed none. If D11 is meant to hold for catalyst closes, this becomes a sweep.
6. **Personas echo across cells. Not MAJOR.** There are 3 instances. The Sage and the Artisan share "drift" and "easier than steering" (W-012). The Artisan's "Unforced" takes the Virtuoso's reworking cost (W-013). The General's "you keep them at your pace" (P127) and the Magistrate's "you keep everyone at your pace" (P131) match, but they never meet on one chart, so they are left as they are.

## 4 · The best and the worst

**Best three lines**
1. "Those who love you know your habits better than your heart." (`[K2 水_比肩 · fn_reading.friction.ledger[0].angles.drive]`, P145, P147.) It is a private cost in plain words, and the line a reader would screenshot. It is better than The Pattern.
2. "Their slower thought ends up as the setup to your joke." (`[K2 水_伤官 · fn_reading.catalyst.ledger[0].angles.cost_others]`, P144.) The cost to the other person is exact and funny, and it carries no charge.

(`[K2 水_正官 · fn_reading.catalyst.ledger[0].angles.cost_others]`, P132.) The syntax is awkward, "course" appears twice, and the close is hedged (W-014).
