## 3 · Systemic patterns

1. **Page-level word echo, driven by fixed shared text (not MAJOR).** 30 of 35 pages repeat a content word ×3 or more. Only P091, P107, P111, P116 and P167 carry joints only or are clean. Most cases involve a chip word (exempt) or the fixed pair head meeting a keyword passage. Examples: P120 "hold" ×4 (the blend definition "held close", "hold things than need holding", "a subject that holds still", the edge "holds for good"); P117 "people" ×4. Where an obvious swap exists it is filed as a TWEAK (MR4-05 to MR4-11). Elsewhere (P113 "someone", P120 "hold", P093 "give") every swap would change a strong line or move the echo, so I left them. C is the only criterion this costs.
2. **Definition tails that reuse the fixed head's verb (not MAJOR; 5 pages).** P107 and P108 say "leaves" again after the head's "before it leaves you" (MR4-03, MR4-04). P101 "you take on little" follows "what you take on", P097 "you claim far more" follows the head's double "claim", and P109 "stand on your own" follows "stands on". The P101 and P109 echoes read as deliberate and are not filed. Writers of a tail should avoid the head's key verb.
3. **A shared remedy diagnosis that fits one persona of the pair (MINOR; 1 clear case, 1 weak).** 金_金's "With no outside measure in view" contradicts the Rival (MR4-02). 土_金's "You give out faster than you refill" fits the Artisan exactly but the Virtuoso only loosely (P104: too direct, talks back, grades people). Even so, it does not contradict the Virtuoso page, so it is not filed.
4. **Friction cost-to-others closes with others as the subject (not MAJOR; 3 fields, 4 pages).** P092 "their side of it comes last", P098 and P099 "They miss the new starts", P116 "They lose days to a decision you had already reached". None is a charge, and each is a flat cost. Only P098/P099 also lacks the owned cause, so only that one is filed (MR4-01).
5. **Cross-persona overlaps inside Metal that no single reader meets (recorded for the cross auditor, not counted).** The chips "Does it properly" (Magistrate, P095/P096) and "Learns it properly" (Sage, P119/P120) share a word and a shape. "… is the only way you know" opens both the Magistrate's "Does it properly" (P096) and the Virtuoso's "Says it straight" (P107). The rising bar belongs to the Virtuoso ("the mark moves each time you near it"), yet it also appears in the General/Magistrate blend's definition ("The bar rises each time you clear it", P093) and the Twin's edge ("the bar you keep raising in private"). One core reads Metal as only one function, so no reader sees two of these together. This is the cross audit's D2 question, not a page defect.

## 4 · The best and the worst

**Best**
1. "You stopped finding errors long ago and now check for comfort." (P115, P117 · `K2 金_偏印 · friction.ledger[0].drive`). The cost and the hidden need fit in eleven plain words, and the reader can test it against their own week. It is better than The Pattern.
2. "You only ever wanted to be believed, and the hurt was never the point." (P104 · `K2 金_伤官 · friction.ledger[0].drive`). It is the BIZ_04 want close done perfectly. It gives back the motive without excusing the cost. The therapy-literate reader screenshots this line.
3. "Those closest to you get you after the show, the shine already spent." (P107 · `K2 金_伤官 · catalyst.ledger[2].cost_others`). The price is stated flat and exactly, with no charge and an image the reader keeps. Also strong: "Your circle is small, and each gap in it was your own call." (P112).

**Weakest**
1. "You keep the people close to you inside the old plan instead of letting them change it. They miss the new starts they wanted to make." (P098, P099 · MR4-01). There is no reason of the reader's own, and the close is about others. It is the only friction passage here without an owned cause.
2. "With no outside measure in view, you answer only to yourself." (P110, P111 · MR4-02). It sits on the same page as "You keep an exact score with everyone you like". The remedy denies the page it cures.
3. "In balance, the test is fair and it ends. What passes it settles in, and you trust it enough to use it." (P170 · MR4-12). It has four pronouns with two referents, so a B1 reader stops to sort them out. It is the one sentence on the Balanced pages that needs a second read.
