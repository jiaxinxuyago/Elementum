## 金_劫财 · The Rival · cost to others (Keeps score)

You measure yourself against the people closest to you, usually without meaning to. Competition is how you stay sharp, and the people you love are the ones nearest the measuring stick. At heart you want a fair fight, and a fair fight needs someone keeping count. The people near you feel it as a distance they cannot name, and over time they stop telling you their wins.

## 土_偏印 · The Alchemist · cost to others (Overprepared)

You are more comfortable getting ready than being judged on what you did. Learning feels like progress and carries no risk of being wrong, so you stay in it longer than the work needs. The people waiting on you experience this as being kept out. They asked for something you could already give, and what they get is another delay you can explain.

## 水_食神 · The Artisan · cost to you (Nourishing)

Taking care of people is where you feel most like yourself. When someone is fed, comforted or entertained by you, you feel useful, and useful is as close as you get to feeling safe. The difficulty is that you rarely let anyone do the same for you. You give until you are empty, and then feel let down by people who were never told you needed anything.

The longer you stay, the more finished the thing has to be before you let it out, which means it never quite is. The people waiting on you asked for something you could already give, and each delay you can explain teaches them to stop asking. What they end up receiving is your standard, not your help.

### 水_食神 · The Artisan · cost to you (Nourishing)

Because the giving is where the safety is, letting someone give back feels like giving the safety up, and you decline it without noticing. Each time you decline, the people around you learn that you need nothing. You end up empty among people who were taught, by you, not to notice.

## Iteration 8 · 2026-10-14 · conciseness: phrases over clauses

The owner's note on the batch 1 examples (all three v0.7.1 lines won over the originals): the sentences run as compounds of two or three clauses. Simplify them into shorter sentences with fewer clauses; prefer a phrase to a clause wherever the clause only carries a logical relation; a trailing "which ..." clause is redundant structure to fold into the main sentence or convert to a phrase (the example: "You trust the version you suffered for more than the one that simply came, which puts a price on what was free.").
