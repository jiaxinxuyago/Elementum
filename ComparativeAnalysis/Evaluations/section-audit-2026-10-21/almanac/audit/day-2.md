## 3 · Systemic patterns

1. **The reader always wins** (D2-M1). 30 of 34 pages and 13 of 13 friction landings close on success. Examples: "You settle it by evening, on your own terms." (punish.day) · "You adjust faster than the people around you." (clash.day). MAJOR.
2. **Superlatives in shared need units** (D2-M2). 9 pages and 6 units, contradicting each other for one reader. Examples: "the energy your chart lacks most" (Fire, D088) · "the energy your chart is shortest of" (Earth, D091), same chart. MAJOR.
3. **Ground lines off the locked verbs, with blind tails** (D2-M3). 33 pages and 5 units. Examples: "which the Earth holds down, and you put both to work" (a released Body, D052) · "and you feel it hold all the way through". MAJOR.
4. **One clause frame per page, three times** (D2-M4). 31 of 34 pages carry ", and you" ×2–3. Examples: D065 ("…and you enjoy the day more than most" / "…and you feel it soften as it goes" / "…and you feel both at once"). D070 has three as well. MAJOR.
5. **Bond-agnostic echo and marker units mis-state the bond or the term** (D2-m1, m2, m3, m5). 5 pages. Examples: "the whole month's clash" on a punish and a harm month (D069, D108) · "the day strikes the same gate" on a repeat (D061). Not MAJOR: four single-unit rewrites.
6. **Content-word pile-ups from headline, landing and reminder sharing a noun** (C). 8 pages over the one-per-page limit. Examples: "plan" ×5 (D057), "work" ×5 (D069), "test" ×4 (D077), "year" ×4 (D092, D097). Partly fixed by D2-t2 and D2-t4. The rest needs the reminder body to avoid the headline's noun. Not MAJOR.

## 5 · The best and the worst

**Best**
1. "You think a step past where thinking helps, and your plans stay on paper today." (`ENERGY mind · day.heavy.b`, D098). The Pattern's flat interior claim, the cost named, no win.
2. "A plan you settled at work comes loose, and someone asks you to defend it. You defend it in fewer words than you expected." (`land.day.clash.month`, D069). Specific, a little surprising, quotable.
3. "Home repeats an old pattern, the same talk over dinner or the same small habit. You see it plainly today and choose which part to keep." (`land.day.repeat.day`, D096). It names a private loop and leaves the reader the choice. This is the shape D2-M1 asks for.

Honourable mention: "Tasks and offers pile up past what you can handle, and you drop the smallest." (`ENERGY action · day.heavy.a`, D108).

**Weakest**
1. "Under it runs Metal, which feeds the Water, and you feel it hold all the way through." (`ground.feeds`). It says nothing a reader could check against their day.
2. "Your new year of age begins today. You start it on your own day, with your own energy at full strength." (`mark.birthday`). False on most birthdays, and the idiom is clumsy.
3. "You feel the whole month's clash come to a head today, because the month presses on the same gate." (`echo.month`). The bond is often wrong and the cause backwards.
