# Audience, Market and Products for Astrology, Personality and Metaphysics Apps (context for Elementum)

Research date: 2026-10-16. Survey data is marked as a survey; press anecdote and company claims are marked as such. Data from before 2022 is flagged [PRE-2022].

## 1. Demographics and size: who uses Co-Star, The Pattern, CHANI, Sanctuary, Nebula, 16Personalities, Enneagram and Human Design apps?

### Takeaway
The best survey evidence (Pew, fielded Oct 2024; published May 2025) shows the US core audience is women under 50, LGBT adults, younger adults, lower-income adults and people without a college degree. About 3 in 10 Americans consult astrology each year, but most say they do it "for fun." The figures for individual apps are mostly company claims from 2019–2022. They show tens of millions of downloads, reached largely by word of mouth, and a skew toward young women (Co-Star: about a quarter of US women aged 18–25 had downloaded it by 2021).

### Cited Findings
**National surveys (US)**
- Pew (survey of 9,593 US adults, Oct 21–27, 2024, American Trends Panel; published May 21, 2025): 28% consult astrology or horoscopes at least once a year, 11% tarot, 6% fortune tellers, and 30% at least one of the three. Use is 6% weekly and 14% monthly. — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew: 27% believe in astrology, against 29% in 2017 (no significant change). By group: women 35% and men 18%. Women 18–49 are at **43%**, women 50+ at 27%, men 18–49 at 20% and men 50+ at 16%. 46% of women aged 18–49 consult astrology yearly. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew: 54% of LGBT adults consult astrology yearly, against 28% of all adults. Within that group, LGBT women are at 63% and LGBT men at 40%. 43% of LGBT adults believe, against 26% of non-LGBT adults. 33% of LGBT adults use tarot yearly, against 9% of non-LGBT adults. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew, by age: about 25% of 18–29s use tarot yearly, against 6% of 50–64s and 3% of those 65+. 14% of under-30s have used a fortune teller, against 2% of those 65+. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew, by income and education: 37% of lower-income adults believe in astrology, against 16% of upper-income adults. Adults without a degree are more likely to believe; Pew gives no exact figure. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew, by race and ethnicity: Asian Americans are the most likely to consult a fortune teller (15%, against 3% of White Americans). Asian Americans also lead on using these practices for major life decisions (12%, against 4% of White Americans). Hispanic Catholics are at 39% yearly astrology use and Black Protestants at 33%. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Gallup (June 2025): about a quarter of Americans believe in astrology. This is cited secondhand via The Conversation. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/astrologys-appeal-in-uncertain-times-264174)
- Social Currents study (Noy, Scheitle, Corcoran, 2025; nationally representative surveys plus 31 interviews): just under half of Americans have ever consulted a horoscope. The figure is more than half of women against slightly over a third of men, and about 60% of sexual minorities against just under half of heterosexuals. Younger adults are consistently more likely to consult. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/astrologys-appeal-in-uncertain-times-264174)

**Individual apps (mostly company claims)**
- Co-Star [PRE-2022]: raised a $15M Series A led by Spark Capital in April 2021 and claimed more than 20M downloads "without any real marketing." A quarter of all US women aged 18–25 had downloaded it. — [Axios, Apr 2021](https://www.axios.com/2021/04/14/astrology-app-co-star-raises-15-million-funding); [TIME, Jul 2021](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- Co-Star: one undated UK report gives 30M registered users, "the majority of whom are young and female." The date is unclear, so treat the figure as a company claim. — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2090394)
- Co-Star [PRE-2022]: raised $5.2M in 2019 with about 11 staff, and had about 3M+ downloads that year. — [Business Insider, 2019](https://www.businessinsider.nl/co-star-iphone-app-popular-with-millennials-is-bringing-astrology-back-2019-4)
- Co-Star launched "Co-Star Plus" in 2022 and has gated more features each year. This comes from an app-comparison blog with a commercial interest; treat it cautiously. — [unstar.app, 2026](https://unstar.app/th/blog/co-star-sanctuary-pattern-nebula-stellium-astrology-apps-ranked-2026)
- The Pattern: had at least 18M profiles and 50% year-on-year growth in active users, "almost entirely without marketing," with 15 employees (Jan 2022). — [Bustle, Jan 2022](https://www.bustle.com/rule-breakers/the-pattern-lisa-donovan-made-the-best-astrology-app). It had about 15M users and 30,000 users through word of mouth before launch [PRE-2022]. — [TIME, Jul 2021](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- Sanctuary: last raised $3M on 5/10/2021, with $4.55M raised in total (Greycroft, KEC Ventures, BITKRAFT and others). Its model is a free horoscope plus paid live chats with astrologers and tarot readers. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/pattern-vs-sanctuary-ventures); [TIME](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- CHANI: self-funded with no venture capital. Inc.'s estimate, relayed by a vendor blog, puts revenue at about $14M a year from an $11.99/month subscription, with 2M+ downloads. This is unverified. — [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/chani-nicholas-chani-astrology-app)
- Nebula: Sensor Tower data cited on Wikipedia says it became the most-downloaded astrology app in the US in summer 2020 and the global leader by summer 2021. Third-party estimates put its monthly revenue at about $516K (Surfshark, May 2025) to about $550K (Screens Design). Neither source gives a clear method. — [Wikipedia: Nebula Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebula_Space); [Surfshark, May 2025](https://surfshark.com/research/chart/astrology-apps-privacy); [Screens Design](https://screensdesign.com/categories/astrology-apps/)
- Category spending [PRE-2022]: in Sensor Tower's analysis, the top 10 astrology apps in the US earned $39.7M in 2019, up 64.7% on 2018. — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/astrology-apps-2019-revenue-downloads)
- Market-size forecasts: The Business Research Company projects the astrology app market at $3.94B (2024) rising to $4.75B (2025), a 20.5% CAGR. This is a vendor forecast with no visible method, and it does not fit the much smaller revenue figures for individual apps. — [GII / TBRC](https://www.gii.tw/report/tbrc1730783-astrology-app-global-market-report.html)
- US "psychic services" market: about $2.2B [PRE-2022]. — [TIME, 2021](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)

### Inferences
- The center of the audience is young women and LGBT adults: women 18–49 believe at 43% and LGBT women consult yearly at 63%. A design-led English-language BaZi app is likely to find its early adopters there first.
- Asian Americans over-index on fortune tellers and on using these practices for decisions. They may be a natural bridge audience for a Chinese-system product.
- Co-Star, The Pattern and CHANI all reached scale through word of mouth and social sharing rather than paid acquisition. In this category, being shareable is the growth engine.
- The lower-income and no-degree skew in belief sits awkwardly with the "design-led" premium positioning. Willingness to pay may come more from deep engagement (e.g. LGBT adults, young women) than from income.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable published user counts or demographics for 16Personalities, Enneagram apps or Human Design apps, and no recent (2024–2026) Sensor Tower or data.ai report on the astrology category.
- No current (2024–2026) audited user figures for Co-Star, The Pattern or CHANI. Most figures are 2021–2022 company claims.
- No YouGov or Harris figures were retrieved in this pass.
- No country-level breakdown of audiences outside the US.

## 2. Motivations: why do Gen Z and millennials use astrology and personality typing?

### Takeaway
Survey and academic evidence agrees that the main use is self-understanding and identity language rather than prediction. Coping with uncertainty, a sense of control, community (especially for LGBTQ+ people) and a light, "for fun" social register come next. Only a small minority uses these practices for major decisions, but that share is much higher among under-30s and LGBT adults.

### Cited Findings
- Pew: 20% of adults use astrology, tarot or fortune tellers mostly "just for fun," against 10% who use them mainly for helpful insights. Among under-30s, nearly 1 in 5 use them for insights, and among LGBT adults 29%. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Pew: 1% rely on these practices "a lot" for major life decisions and 5% "a little." The share rises to 13% of under-30s, 21% of LGBT adults, 11% of lower-income adults and 12% of Asian Americans. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Social Currents (2025): astrology is "less about predicting the future and more about making sense of the self in an uncertain world." Most participants treat it as entertainment or as a personality lens, often comparing it to Myers-Briggs or the Enneagram, and few see it as literally predictive. The authors say it offers identity language, a sense of control and community, especially for LGBTQ+ people. Professional astrologers frame it closer to therapy: "not deterministic, but a source of guidance and assurance." — [The Conversation, Sep 2025](https://theconversation.com/astrologys-appeal-in-uncertain-times-264174)
- TIME [PRE-2022, 2021] gives several drivers, all from journalism and interviews rather than surveys: personalization beyond the sun sign, astrology as self-care or therapy, building a "digital identity" (trend forecaster Lucie Greene), pandemic-era career uncertainty, and a move from organized religion to "self-directed spirituality." A user quote: "It felt like a handbook, like, Oh, this is who you are." — [TIME](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- The Pattern's founder: "every person wants to feel seen and understood." She says it is "a life app," not an astrology app (founder statement). — [Bustle, 2022](https://www.bustle.com/rule-breakers/the-pattern-lisa-donovan-made-the-best-astrology-app)
- A 2023 qualitative study of spiritual apps (Przydatek) found people use them for personal development, hope, mental health and self-reflection. I saw this only through a secondary summary. — cited in search results; no primary link verified.
- A Duquesne University thesis on millennial women (small qualitative sample) describes astrology as giving "a highly individualized sense of self-cohesion," community and reassurance. — [Duquesne Scholarship Collection](https://dsc.duq.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3262&context=etd)
- A commercial survey from EduBirdie (2,000 Americans aged 16–34, not peer-reviewed) says 80% believe in astrology at least some of the time and claims over half use it for important decisions. This conflicts sharply with Pew's 13% of under-30s, so treat it as low quality. — [PRWeb / EduBirdie](https://www.prweb.com/releases/edubirdie-survey-reveals-over-half-of-young-americans-rely-on-astrology-to-make-important-life-decisions-302237674.html); contradicted by [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- China, from press and interviews rather than surveys: anxiety over jobs, exams, love and money; a sense of control; therapy being stigmatized and hard to get; and a "last generation" pessimism. Youth unemployment was 21% in June 2023 and 16% at end-2024. — [MIT Technology Review, Mar 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/03/1112604/deepseek-fortune-teller-china/); [Taipei Times / AFP, Jul 2025](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2025/07/03/2003839646)
- The Barnum effect: people rate vague, mostly positive and seemingly personalized descriptions as highly accurate. Forer's 1948 generic profile included a hedged weakness ("you can generally compensate"). This is a psychological mechanism, not a motivation, but it explains why "this is so me" happens. — [Wikipedia: Barnum effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect); [Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/barnum-effect)

### Inferences
- Elementum's personality-reading framing (self-understanding rather than prediction) matches the main motivation the research documents. "Who you are" content beats "what will happen" content for this audience.
- Two registers sit side by side: a majority who use it "for fun" and socially, and a sizeable minority, concentrated among young and LGBT users, who use it for real guidance. The product needs to work in both: light enough to share, deep enough to sit with.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed quantitative study from 2022–2026 specifically on why people use astrology *apps*, as opposed to astrology in general.
- No motivation data specific to MBTI, Enneagram or Human Design users in Western markets.

## 3. The Chinese and East Asian metaphysics wave (玄学, BaZi, MBTI) and English-language BaZi apps

### Takeaway
China has a large and growing youth "xuanxue economy," driven by anxiety and economic pressure and now channeled through AI (DeepSeek BaZi readings) and apps such as 测测/Cece. MBTI has become a social identity label among Chinese youth. English-language BaZi apps exist (Mingora, BaZi Hero, FateAlert, AI tools like Fatetell), but they are small and early. I found no data showing scaled Western demand.

### Cited Findings
- A Chinese Academy of Social Sciences survey (2025) found 68% of people aged 18–35 believe in at least one xuanxue principle. This is cited via Daxue Consulting, and no method is published. — [Daxue Consulting, Aug 2025](https://daxueconsulting.com/spiritual-economy/)
- iiMedia Research: China's AI metaphysics market is above RMB 12B (about $1.67B), growing 43.7% a year, and more than 70% of users are aged 18–30. — [Daxue Consulting](https://daxueconsulting.com/spiritual-economy/)
- On Xiaohongshu, the #玄学 hashtag had 4.5B views and 65.6M posts as of July 2025. Bilibili hosts "mass divination" (大众占卜) tarot videos. Products include temple bracelets, amulets and "luck-changing" (转运) jewelry. — [Daxue Consulting](https://daxueconsulting.com/spiritual-economy/)
- China's "temple economy" was RMB 90B in 2023. — [Daxue Consulting](https://daxueconsulting.com/spiritual-economy/)
- Bloomberg (June 2025) headline: "China's Spiritual Economy Booms as Gen Z Turns to Tarot, Astrology." Dashway Consulting's estimate of about $19B is quoted only through a social-media summary and is unverified. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-20/china-s-spiritual-economy-booms-as-gen-z-turns-to-tarot-astrology)
- Aurora Mobile reports that Gen Z is now the main buyer of metaphysical products and services. This is secondhand. — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016792)
- 测测 (Cece), a Tencent-backed astrology app: more than 100M downloads per AFP/Taipei Times, but about 24M per CNN citing Sensor Tower. The figures conflict, and the Sensor Tower figure is likely the more rigorous. — [Taipei Times, Jul 2025](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2025/07/03/2003839646); [CNN, Jul 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/07/style/young-chinese-spiritual-luxury-xuanxue-intl-hnk-dst) (CNN figure from search snippet; page not retrievable)

### Inferences
- Chinese youth treat BaZi much as Western youth treat birth charts: an identity and anxiety-management tool, now delivered through AI. That suggests the motivations carry across cultures.
- English-language BaZi apps are crowded with small calculators and AI-report sellers, and no breakout brand is visible. A design-led, voice-driven product (the Co-Star/Pattern formula applied to BaZi) appears to be an open position. This is an inference, not a measured gap.
- MBTI's role in China as social currency (an ice-breaker, a dating filter) shows that a compact, shareable label is what spreads. A Day Master or element type can play the same role.

### Gaps

## 4. What content styles win: depth, accuracy, voice, shareability, willingness to pay

### Takeaway
The winners pair a distinctive voice with personalization beyond the sun sign: Co-Star is blunt and meme-able, The Pattern is calm and jargon-free and "frighteningly accurate," and CHANI is warm and self-accepting. They grew through screenshots and word of mouth. Monetization is freemium: a free daily hook, with paid depth (compatibility reports, full charts, subscriptions of about $12 a month, one-off reports of about $40).

### Cited Findings
- Co-Star's push notifications are "brutal" and have become a "cultural meme." Users screenshot and post them, and the app's "daily dose of tough love" circulates as viral tweets. — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/co-star-astrology-app-push-notifications-memes); [Masters of Media, UvA, 2020](https://mastersofmedia.hum.uva.nl/2020/09/co-star-personalized-astrology-as-a-form-of-communication) [PRE-2022]
- Co-Star pairs "hard" science claims ("A.I.-powered," "NASA data") with uncannily specific readings. — [Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/54991-costar-astrology-app-how-it-works) [PRE-2022]
- An app-comparison blog with a commercial interest says Co-Star's brand-voice notifications "are great when you get one a week," implying fatigue at higher frequency. — [unstar.app, 2026](https://unstar.app/th/blog/co-star-sanctuary-pattern-nebula-stellium-astrology-apps-ranked-2026)
- The Pattern ("the best astrology app for skeptics"): no sun-sign astrology and no symbols ("there's no Saturn"). It uses short, swipeable slides in a calm, therapist-like voice with a non-mystical blue and green interface. Features include compatibility checks with friends and crushes and a dating feature, Connect. — [Bustle, 2022](https://www.bustle.com/rule-breakers/the-pattern-lisa-donovan-made-the-best-astrology-app); [TIME](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- The Pattern has a reputation for accuracy: reviews call it "frighteningly accurate," and Channing Tatum and Jennifer Aniston have reportedly praised it. This comes from lifestyle press and is anecdotal. — [Women's Weekly SG](https://www.womensweekly.com.sg/your-life/astrology-apps-daily-readings-birth-chart-readings-horoscope)
- Sanctuary's voice goal, in the founder's words: "like you're texting your friend who happens to be this amazing astrologer." — [TIME](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- CHANI is positioned around "radical self-acceptance," self-discovery, mindfulness and healing. It has a free daily horoscope with birth-chart depth behind a paywall at $11.99 a month. — [Webby Awards](https://www.webbyawards.com/s9-ep2-chani-nicholas-astrologer-and-author); [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/chani-nicholas-chani-astrology-app); [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/chaninicholas/)
- Compatibility reports are central to what Co-Star offers and how it planned to make money [PRE-2022]. — [TIME](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- Willingness to pay: the top 10 US astrology apps earned $39.7M in 2019 [PRE-2022]. Nebula's estimated revenue is about $0.5M a month (2025). CHANI's is about $14M a year (Inc. estimate). AI BaZi full reports sell at $39.99 (Fatetell). — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/astrology-apps-2019-revenue-downloads); [Surfshark](https://surfshark.com/research/chart/astrology-apps-privacy); [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/chani-nicholas-chani-astrology-app); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/03/1112604/deepseek-fortune-teller-china/)
- In China, astrology and tarot bloggers on Xiaohongshu and Bilibili post daily horoscopes and recommend lucky items. Content often ends in commerce (bracelets, crystals), and DeepSeek BaZi readings often recommend buying crystals or jewelry. — [Daxue Consulting](https://daxueconsulting.com/spiritual-economy/); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/03/1112604/deepseek-fortune-teller-china/)
- Personality-quiz content spreads on TikTok through videos of people sharing their results (color personality test examples). — [NBC Philadelphia](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/tiktok-users-cant-get-enough-of-this-color-personality-test/2726918/); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/viral-personality-colour-quiz)
- Barnum effect: vagueness, mostly positive wording, apparent authority and a sense of being personally addressed all raise perceived accuracy. — [Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/barnum-effect); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect)

### Inferences
- Shareable content has three traits: it is short and quotable, it is specific to the person, and it carries an emotional edge (funny-cutting at Co-Star, uncanny at The Pattern). A screenshot-sized line is the unit that spreads.
- Accuracy here means feeling seen, and voice is how a product earns it: the products praised for depth all name a recognizable inner tension, not only virtues.
- A credible premium path for a BaZi product is a free identity reading plus paid compatibility and deeper period or yearly readings. This mirrors Co-Star's compatibility reports, CHANI's subscription and Fatetell's one-off report.

### Gaps
- I found no measured data on screenshot or share rates, conversion rates or retention for any app.
- No sourced statistics on the "this is too accurate" viral posts for The Pattern; the evidence is only anecdotal press.
- No current pricing confirmed for Co-Star Plus, The Pattern or Nebula.

## 5. How these products handle negative traits at the product level

### Takeaway
The products span a range. At one end, Co-Star is blunt and sometimes bleak ("You are a constellation of sadness"), and that bluntness is part of its meme appeal. In the middle, The Pattern names vulnerabilities and inadequacies calmly, then turns to "now what do I do?". At the soft end, Sanctuary and CHANI are upbeat or self-accepting. Personality tests and Barnum research suggest weaknesses are accepted best when they are hedged and paired with a way to compensate. Grim, fatalistic predictions (as in some AI BaZi readings) disturb users and are discounted.

### Cited Findings
- Co-Star: a notification told a TIME writer "You are a constellation of sadness," and a compatibility report warned she would often feel unloved. TIME calls Co-Star "the bluntest." — [TIME, 2021](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/) [PRE-2022]

### Inferences
- Bluntness can work when it is witty, short and about today, as with Co-Star's daily line: it reads as play and gets shared. The same bluntness about who you are permanently, or about fate (health, divorce), lands as harm.
- The model most transferable to a reading about who someone is appears to be The Pattern's: name the tension precisely and without jargon, keep the tone calm, and move to "now what." This fits Elementum's structure of a friction page with a remedy line.
- Weaknesses framed as the cost of a strength, with a way to compensate, match the Barnum mechanism that drives perceived accuracy and acceptance. This is inference from psychology research, not product A/B data.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party statements, design docs or research from 16Personalities or Enneagram apps on how they write weaknesses, and no user research on reactions to them.
- No quantitative data (reviews analysis, churn) linking negative framing to retention or sharing.
- Co-Star's editorial guidelines and the writer's process (a Goodreads-hosted "Job Profile" exists) were not retrieved: [Goodreads blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/19561483-job-profile-the-writer-behind-those-chaotic-co-star-notifications).
