# Competitor onboarding and first-reveal research

Date: 2026-11-05. Scope: The Pattern, Nebula, 16Personalities, Cece, FateTell. Method: public web pages only (store listings, product sites, Screensdesign teardowns, press, review aggregators). Confidence: **H** = from the product's own page or store listing, read today. **M** = from a reputable third party, or a self-reported figure. **L** = an estimate, a single anecdote, or an aggregator that conflicts with other sources.

Gaps to know about: no Reddit thread came up for any product; in-app screens behind a login (Nebula's placement explainers, The Pattern's full trait text, FateTell's first FateBook card) are not published, so where this report gives their copy it comes from press quotes.

---

## 1. The Pattern (Pattern Home, Inc.)

**Onboarding.** Screensdesign's screen-by-screen capture shows: splash, then sign up / log in, then four sign-in options, then email entry and verification, then **one profile screen asking for name, birth date, birth time and gender**, then birth location (search plus map), then a "Review Details" screen with two consent boxes and the button **"View My Pattern"**, then a notification prompt, a carousel of insight cards, and a paywall ("Access Go Deeper+", yearly preselected). There is no quiz and no explanation of astrology at any point (https://screensdesign.com/apps/the-pattern/). A Pratt design critique notes that "Skip for now" on the birth-time step "is noticeably hard to discern as a button" (https://ixd.prattsi.org/2023/02/design-critique-the-pattern/).

**First reveal.** The first thing users get is a list of personality traits with plain-English titles; no planet or sign names appear. Titles reported by reviewers include "Confident & Kind", "Sensitive & Over Giving", "Relationship Blocks", "Ideal Relationship", "Destiny" and "Work & Public Life" (https://www.pedestrian.tv/health/the-pattern-astrology-app/), along with "restless or fearful" and "responsible and over-giving" (https://www.trueself.com/the-pattern-astrology-app-2639898914). Bustle quotes the opening of one first reading: **"You're a true individual — uniquely yourself… Whether you're comfortable with it or not, you're different."** (https://www.bustle.com/life/pattern-app-review-features-price). I could not find the text of a "Foundation" section published anywhere.

**Explaining the system.** It doesn't explain it. The App Store listing says it is "Created to help you feel seen and understood" and "acts as a mirror" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-pattern/id1071085727). A student reviewer: "There's no difficult terms for people new to astrology… user-friendly descriptions of your personality instead" (https://hilltopviewsonline.com/19784/life-and-arts/love-astrology-but-struggle-with-its-vocab-this-app-might-be-for-you).

**Emotional techniques.**
- Trait titles that pair a strength with a struggle ("Sensitive & Over Giving").
- Being "different" framed as identity ("Whether you're comfortable with it or not").
- Hidden wounds named directly ("wounding in relationships", per Autostraddle: https://www.autostraddle.com/ruin-your-day-and-your-relationship-with-new-astrology-app-the-pattern-435133/).
- Comparing traits with friends.
- Timed "cycles" with countdowns ("9 months long, 260 days left").

**Reviewers.**
- "scarily accurate"; "felt spot-on for me" (Bustle).
- "utterly seen" (Pedestrian).
- "surprisingly accurate. Not just once in a while, constantly" (lidamoonrose, App Store US).
- "I was so blown away by how deeply accurate and well written the information was" (StarburstCA, App Store US).
- Negative: "more generalised with maybe one or two sentences that are different to others in your sun sign" (App Store reviewer, via Screensdesign search snippet); "can repeat the same points" (nighttimewanderlust, App Store US).
- Autostraddle's captions: "Call me the fuck out", "But YOU understand me, don't you?!?!?"

**Data.**
- App Store US: 4.0 from 16K ratings. **H**
- "Trusted by 8M+ users" (listing). **M**, self-reported.
- Google Play: about 3.8 stars, 24.8K reviews, about 1M installs (apppricinglab.com/app/google_play/com.thepattern.app). **L**; AppGoblin shows 3.8M installs, which conflicts.
- Screensdesign estimates $350k a month in revenue. **L**

## 2. Nebula: Horoscope & Astrology (Obrio Limited)

**Onboarding.** This is the longest funnel in the set: about 30 screens and about 2.5 minutes before the paywall (https://screensdesign.com/apps/nebula-horoscope-astrology/). In order:
1. Tracking (ATT) prompt.
2. Goals quiz, then a goals confirmation.
3. "Main reason" quiz.
4. Value-proposition screens.
5. Date of birth, then time of birth (with "I Don't Know"), then place of birth.
6. "Do you want guidance from horoscopes?"
7. Testimonial.
8. Gender, explained as being for relationship and well-being readings.
9. Name.
10. Testimonial.
11. Relationship status (3x3 grid, from "Single" to "Complicated").
12. Life interests (Money, Family, Career…).
13. Compatibility value prop ("Explore how you will express yourselves together"; "Check to see if your intuition is confirmed").
14. Testimonial.
15. Notification set-up.
16. "How did you hear about us".
17. Before-and-after comparison.
18. Account creation.
19. **Profile-creation loading screen with a progress bar and a testimonial.**
20. Paywall (weekly, 3-month or yearly; 3-day trial), then a $19.99 birth-chart upsell.

Screensdesign calls this "a masterclass in gradual user investment."

**First reveal.** The home screen opens on your **Sun, Moon and Ascendant**, plus the Sun sign's element, polarity and modality, above daily, weekly, monthly and yearly horoscopes and love, career and health percentages (https://sweetyhigh.com/read/nebula-horoscope-astrology-app-review-032521; https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/nebula-review).

**Explaining the system.** Mindbodygreen describes "easy-to-comprehend explainers on your chart placements." The explainer wording itself is not public. The store copy stresses that "Every horoscope and astrology insight… is based on your entire birth chart" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nebula-horoscope-astrology/id1459969523).

**Emotional techniques.**
- Asking about relationship status and life goals before the reveal, so the reading can mirror them back.
- Three testimonials placed inside the funnel.
- The web funnel's claim of "93% accuracy, rated by real users" (https://appnebula.co/).
- Compatibility pitched as "check to see if your intuition is confirmed."

**Reviewers.**
- "Crazy accurate!!! I am not sure who writes these" (App Store US).
- "stunningly accurate given the slightly clumsy, out-of-focus morning I've had" (Sweety High).
- "a few of the insights were very spot on" but "more of a fun activity" (Mindbodygreen).
- Negative: "vague, pre-written fluff" (Medium, as quoted in search results).
- Billing complaints dominate the negative feedback ("unexpectedly billed $64…", unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/nebula).

**Data.**
- App Store US: 4.6 from 171K ratings. **H**
- "Community of over 12 million" (App Store "What's New"). **M**
- "20 million happy users" (appnebula.co). **M**; the two self-reported counts conflict.
- 900 to 1,000+ psychic advisors. **M**

## 3. 16Personalities (NERIS Analytics)

**Onboarding.** A free web test, pitched as "Only 10 minutes to get a 'freakishly accurate' description of who you are and why you do things the way you do" (https://www.16personalities.com/). No account or birth data is needed. The question count and answer scale were not visible in what I could fetch (the test runs in JavaScript). A secondhand screenshot shows the result opens with the type name plus variant ("Defender ISFJ-T") and a "My traits" chart of percentages for each of the five scales (https://www.lemon8-app.com/@ray_dae/7378621247585845765). **L**

**First reveal (INFJ page, quoted).** It has three layers:
- **The nickname:** "Advocate".
- **A one-line tagline:** "Advocates are quiet visionaries, often serving as inspiring and tireless idealists."
- **The opening paragraph:** "Idealistic and principled, people with the INFJ personality type (Advocates) aren't content to coast through life – they want to stand up and make a difference. For these compassionate personalities, success doesn't come from money or status but from seeking fulfillment, helping others, and being a force for good in the world."

The page continues: "INFJ personalities might come across as somewhat reserved, but they are fueled by a profound internal passion." The "Seeking Purpose" section then says: "INFJs tend to carry around a sense – whether conscious or not – of being different from most people… they sometimes feel misunderstood or at odds with the world." The Introduction alone is about 750 words, split into four short headed sections with pull-quotes and a Goethe epigraph (https://www.16personalities.com/infj-personality).

**Explaining the system.** Each letter is spelled out as a word beside the code (I = Introverted, N = Intuitive, F = Feeling, J = Judging). Each of the five scales is defined in one plain sentence, for example Introverted/Extraverted: "How people direct and receive energy from their surroundings and social world" (https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai.md). On the current site this scale is labelled "Energy"; older versions called it "Mind". The 16 types are grouped into 4 Roles (Analysts, Diplomats…), so there is a middle layer between "one of 16" and the user. Every type gets a nickname that works as an archetype, with a one-line descriptor ("Bold, imaginative and strong-willed leaders, always finding a way – or making one").

**Emotional techniques.**
- **Contrast:** "might come across as somewhat reserved, but… fueled by a profound internal passion."
- **Naming hidden struggles:** "feel misunderstood", "don't always feel appreciated in return", "very sensitive to criticism", and burnout from caring for others.
- **Rarity framing:** "Perhaps because their personality type is so uncommon".
- **A permission-giving close:** "they need to pause sometimes and take care of themselves as well."
- **Third person, not "you".** It reads as an outside description of "people like you", which may feel less like flattery.

**Reviewers.** These are site testimonials, and the site notes that reviewers receive a reward for leaving feedback.
- "Like someone finally gets me and I'm not a weirdo" (Nicole, INFJ-T).
- "It's like someone putting a mirror to your face" (Benny, INTJ-A).
- "The accuracy of this profile was almost unnerving at first" (Roald).

Critics point to the Barnum effect and to people getting a different type on a retest (https://www.sdfi.co.kr/news/an-accuracy-review-of-the-16-personalities-test.html). A Reddit commenter, quoted secondhand, called the descriptions "too flowery."

**Data (homepage, today).**
- 1.59B+ total tests taken. **H** as a claim, self-reported.
- 322K+ tests taken today; 200M+ in the US. **H** as a claim.
- "91.2% accuracy rating". **M**; this is a self-reported user rating, not validation.
- 45+ languages. **H**

## 4. Cece (测测)

**Which app.** No Western astrology app called "Cece" turned up. The match is **测测 / Cece**, a Chinese app for astrology, MBTI and emotional support. Its App Store ID is 756771906, the developer is Beijing Light For You Technology Co., Ltd., and the operator is 心言集团 (Xinyan Group). Rest of World describes it as backed by Tencent (https://restofworld.org/2023/tencent-cece-spirituality-app/). Other candidates:
- **Ceerly**, "Astrology&Tarot Reading:Ceerly" (apps.apple.com/app/id6745674137; the listing returned 404 today).
- **八字测测专业版** ("BaZi Ce Ce Pro"), a small free BaZi tool with 3 ratings (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1167347029).

**Onboarding.** Users "register by entering their birth time and location," which drives zodiac and astrology readings (Rest of World). The home screen is a grid of entry points: MBTI, zodiac, star chart (星盘), compatibility (缘分合盘), 3D sandplay, and companion and venting features. One prompt reads "输入生日，更加了解TA" ("Enter a birthday, understand them better") (https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/6391148.html). A step-by-step first-run flow is not documented publicly.

**Explaining the system.** The woshipm teardown found almost no help for beginners. The AI's preset questions assume the user knows astrology, and features such as context tags and the deep-thinking button have no explanation. A user on Tencent's app store said "普通人可能看不懂里面的一些专业术语" ("ordinary people may not understand some of the technical terms") (https://sj.qq.com/appdetail/com.xxwolo.cc5/review).

**Emotional techniques.**
- The slogan "遇见更好的自己" ("meet a better version of yourself").
- Relationship-first prompts (analyse a WeChat friend, compatibility checks).
- An always-on AI companion that users nickname "电子闺蜜" ("digital bestie").
- One user's view: "a way for me to seek meaning and solace in this absurd world" (Rest of World).

**Reviewers.**
- Positive: "测的挺准的" ("pretty accurate") and "准是挺准" ("accurate, for sure").
- Negative: "算的不准" ("inaccurate") and "白花钱" ("a waste of money") (Tencent app store).

**Data.**
- About 55M registered users at end-2025. **M**; company figure reported by woshipm, which could not verify it.
- Over 1M monthly active users in 2021. **M**; Rest of World.
- App Store 4.4 from 39.9K ratings and 25M+ downloads. **L**; mwm.ai estimate.
- US storefront: 4.2 from 362 ratings. **H**

## 5. FateTell (AuraAI Limited / FateTell, Inc.)

**Onboarding.** On the web, the homepage form sits right under the hero line "Decode your BaZi, shape your Fate". It asks for:
- birth date;
- birth time, with "I don't know my birth time";
- gender;
- birthplace time zone (a list of cities);
- whether you were born in the Southern Hemisphere.

The button is "View my BaZi chart". Three links next to the form explain the basics: "Day Master: find your Day stem", "Five Elements: counts and relationships" and "Ten Gods" (https://fatetell.com/). On iOS, the version 1.6.3 notes say onboarding lets you "create a profile, then discover your Zodiac, Day Master, Fortune Trend, and reminders one step at a time" (https://apps.apple.com/app/id6752552096). The paid FateBook comes as "10 cards and thousands of words."

**First reveal.** On the web, the first result is a calculated chart: Four Pillars in Chinese characters, the Day Master and element counts. The worked example on the site reads "Day Master: 戊 … Wood 1 · Fire 3 · Earth 3 · Metal 0 · Water 1" (https://fatetell.com/bazi/day-master). The personality wording comes later, inside FateBook, which you can preview with a fictional sample (https://fatetell.com/ai-fortune-teller).

**Explaining the system.** Each Day Master gets one natural-image metaphor:
- "Jia is Yang Wood. Its familiar image is a large tree, with an upright trunk supporting branches that spread outward."
- "Ding is Yin Fire. Think of an oil lamp lighting the space nearby."
- "Wu is Yang Earth, represented by a thick wall that stands firmly and marks a boundary."
- "Geng… an axe or hammer"; "Xin is Yin Metal, pictured as a fine blade."
- "Gui is Yin Water, pictured as fine rain settling on plants."

Each image is hedged straight away: "they do not establish someone's personality or life experience." Much of the content is mechanics: generating and controlling cycles, hidden stems, why counts are not strength. The sample page asks readers to test the reading against their own life: "Compare an interpretation with situations you know: how you handle a deadline, respond to disagreement or choose between familiar work and a new role." The brand guide uses a tree metaphor for the whole system: destiny is "the root blueprint of your life-tree… whether you stand grounded as an oak, endure like a pine, or bloom with the beauty and thorns of a rose", and fortune is the changing seasons (https://fatetell.com/aboutUs).

**Emotional techniques.** In the product these are restrained. The pitch is accuracy and lack of flattery: the site criticises other AI tools for "pandering" and says human reviewers check readings. Sample questions on the site name hidden struggles: "What patterns do I repeat when closeness becomes difficult?" and "Where do I tend to overextend…?"

**Reviewers.**
- "surprisingly detailed about their personality, strengths, and natural tendencies" (JamesHook1, App Store).
- "Definitely the best digital fortune teller I've used!" (tajnning).
- A homepage testimonial: "80% of its content is very accurate."
- No independent review that reports a personal reaction was found; toolcenter.ai describes features only.

**Data.**
- App Store: 4.6 from 342 ratings today. **H**; a search snippet from earlier this year showed 46 ratings at 4.83.
- "100,000+ users worldwide" (site). **M**
- About page: "over 15,000 registered users" after launching in late December 2024. **M**, and older.
- Annual plan $59.99; FateBook $39.99. **H**

## Cross-product findings

### 1. What produces the "this is me" moment in the first minute

The techniques below show up in the products that reviewers most often call "accurate". This is a pattern across reviews, not a measured effect.

- **Name a hidden struggle, not just a strength.** The Pattern pairs them in the trait title ("Sensitive & Over Giving"). 16Personalities follows a compliment with "feel misunderstood" and "sensitive to criticism". Reviewers respond to the struggle part: "Call me the fuck out", "not a weirdo".
- **Contrast between how others see you and how you are inside.** 16Personalities: "might come across as somewhat reserved, but… fueled by a profound internal passion."
- **Make being different part of the identity.** "Whether you're comfortable with it or not, you're different" (The Pattern). "A sense… of being different from most people" (16P). Both are broadly true Barnum statements that read as personal.
- **Relationships early.** Nebula asks about relationship status during onboarding. The Pattern shows "Relationship Blocks" and "Ideal Relationship" on the home screen. Cece leads with analysing a friend.
- **Invest the user first, then reveal.** Nebula uses about 30 screens and a loading bar. 16P uses a 10-minute test. The Pattern uses a "View My Pattern" button. Effort before the result appears to raise belief in it; this is Screensdesign's reading, not measured.
- **Social proof right at the reveal.** 16P's "freakishly accurate" and "1.59B+ tests"; Nebula's testimonials and "93% accuracy".

### 2. How the best products make an unfamiliar system understandable

- **Translate the label into a plain word right away.** 16P never shows "I" without "Introverted", and each scale gets one sentence about everyday behaviour ("how people direct and receive energy"). The Pattern removes the system's terms completely, with no planets. Nebula keeps the jargon and adds explainers. Cece keeps the jargon and gets "can't understand the terms" complaints.
- **Give a human nickname alongside the code.** "INFJ" becomes "Advocate", and a one-line descriptor follows ("quiet visionaries…"). This is the nearest equivalent to "The Blade": a name the user can say out loud, followed by one sentence of who that person is.
- **Use one concrete image per label, then link it to behaviour.** FateTell's images work well (an oil lamp lighting the space nearby; a fine blade), but it stops at the image and adds a disclaimer. No competitor goes the extra step of linking image, then trait, then a "you might notice" moment. For "The Blade" (Xin Metal), the published pattern would be:
  - the image: a fine blade;
  - the quality: precision and an eye for what is off;
  - an everyday example in the style of FateTell's "how you handle a deadline, respond to disagreement".

  This is a gap Elementum could own.
- **A middle layer between system and self.** 16P's four Roles and Nebula's element, polarity and modality give the label a family, so "Earth is your Mind energy" has a home before the user meets the detail.
- **Invite people to check the reading against their own life.** FateTell's "Compare an interpretation with situations you know… Keep the parts that fit your experience" puts the user in charge of the accuracy test. This suits sceptical 25 to 35-year-olds.

### 3. Length and density of the first reveal

- **The Pattern:** a stack of short trait cards with headline-style titles and a few short paragraphs each, with no system terms. It is light to begin with, and people can scroll much further.
- **Nebula:** a dashboard showing three placements and their properties, a daily horoscope and percentage meters. It is dense but fragmented, and the depth sits behind the paywall and the $19.99 report.
- **16Personalities:** the heaviest single reveal. It runs from nickname to one line to an Introduction of about 750 words in 4 headed sections, then 6 or more more sections (strengths, relationships, careers). The text is scannable, with pull-quotes.
- **Cece:** a feature grid rather than a single reveal. There is no single "you are" moment.
- **FateTell:** the first web result is a technical chart of characters and counts, with almost no personality wording. The narrative arrives later, in a paid 10-card FateBook of "thousands of words". Of the five, it has the longest gap between giving data and getting a felt "this is me".
