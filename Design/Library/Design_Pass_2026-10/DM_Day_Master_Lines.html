What the card should say about the Day Master

The share card is read by two people: the owner of the chart, who wants to feel recognised, and a stranger in a feed, who has three seconds and no frame. The line under the name has to do both jobs. Today it carries the who line (“You are the Blade, the Metal that has already been through fire…”), which is a frame, not a trait. Below: what works on cards people actually share, what the station already holds for every stem, and the rewrites, with 庚 The Blade worked through.

1 · What travels on a shared card

The cards that get posted (the big personality tests, the astrology apps, the year-end wraps) share a grammar. A name. Two or three plain words. One sentence in the second person that is concrete enough to be checked against the person. And, the one that travels furthest, a line about how other people use you, because the audience is other people.

  • Recognisable in three seconds. A trait the reader can test against the person (“People come to you when they need the unsoftened truth”), not a metaphor that needs the app to decode (“Precision before intention”).
  • Second person, concrete verbs. “You say what others soften” beats “Direct”. Adjectives alone were retired from the plate in September as generic, and that ruling still reads right: three words fit any of ten people.
  • One cost line earns trust. A trait with its price (“…then quietly pay for being the one who did”) reads as true; pure praise reads as a horoscope.
  • A social line is the share hook. “People come to you for X” is the sentence a friend screenshots back. Half the station’s claim pairs already are one.
  • No invented metrics. There is no honest number for a stem; the five shares already are the card’s numbers. A “directness 87%” meter would be decoration.
