▸§0

IA shift — Today / Energy Map / Guidance / Friends / Profile

Revises DES_04 §11 — five tabs, not eight cards.

DES_04 §11 originally specified a single dashboard tab with an 8-card menu (DayMaster Hero + catalogue grid). v3 collapses this into a five-tab dashboard with the chart's reading content lifted up to the surface of Energy Map. The 8-card catalogue is retired in v1.7+; its detail pages survive as drill-downs (§7). All five tabs share the same persistent bottom-tab nav (v2 §4 — token-locked).

Before · DES_04 §11 v1.7
  1. Single dashboard surface
  2. DayMasterHero, scaled large
  3. 8 catalogue cards · each is a tap target
  4. 6 of 8 readings hidden behind a tap
  5. No persistent navigation chrome
  6. Today as a sub-card, not a primary surface
→
After · v3 IA
  1. Today — daily utility, 0 taps to value
  2. Energy Map — reading dashboard, content-rich
  3. Guidance — premium hub (5 cards)
  4. Friends — compatibility, V1 coming-soon
  5. Profile — minimal · birth data + account
  6. Persistent 5-tab bar across all five

What survives from v1.7. DayMasterHero still exists, scaled smaller, sitting above the identity ribbon on Energy Map. DetailShell, getSections() page-routing, and the lock-blur affordance are all retained for drill-downs into Catalyst, Resistance, Seasonal Calibration, Life Chapters, and Chart Patterns (§7). What changes is the home layout — readings now read inline.

Why the change. The card-menu pattern hid the user's reading behind a tap. Each tab becomes a single concern: Today is a utility (the habit-forming surface), Energy Map is a reading dashboard (the depth surface), Guidance is the premium hub (the upgrade surface), Friends is the social mechanic (the viral surface), and Profile is the account ledger (the trust surface).

▸§10

Tier-lock pattern · in situ

v2 §9 lock-blur applied across Today, Map, Guidance — three densities.

Tier locks come in three densities, ordered by how much of the surrounding surface they consume. Inline (a single row, in flow), card-veil (a whole card, blurred under a translucent silk overlay), and section-veil (a whole section of the page, gradient-faded to a CTA). Density rises with the value of what's behind the lock — a paragraph, a card, an entire feature.

Used for low-friction, low-stakes locks — preview rows, single forces. Never blurs content (there's no content yet to blur).

★ Seeker
Unlock

Used on Life Chapters · partial-list reveal (showing pillars 1–3, fading pillars 4–8). The gradient does the work of the lock.

Density rules

  • A · inline-rowsingle row · gold-tinted background rgba(212,175,55,0.10) + 1px gold-25 border · ★ Unlock as tracked-caps tertiary, never a button
  • B · card-veilv2 §9 lock primitive · 3px blur on the card content · 78% silk overlay · centred CTA pair (tier eyebrow + bronze pill)
  • C · section-veilvertical gradient that fades content into silk in the lower 65% · CTA floats over the fade · only at the bottom of a list, never mid-list
  • Tier hintalways EB caps 9.5 ls 1.8 in --bronzeDark for Seeker, --advisor (#7a5e9a) for Advisor — never inline body copy
  • Forbiddenno full-screen modal interruption · no banner toasts for paywall · no dark scrim — Elementum pays no homage to "subscribe-or-leave"
Composes: v2 §9 lock states · v2 §5C upgrade-sheet (modal C).

▸§14

Drift log v3 — open questions

Six items. Each requires a v1 / v2 owner answer before v3 is sealed.

Items where v3 composition stretched the v1+v2 vocabulary. Each is named, scoped, and proposes a resolution path. None are resolved unilaterally in v3 — they wait on the legend owners.

D14.1 — Decade-card gold rim has no v1 counterpart
Today's decade-pillar card uses a 1px gold rim (rgba(212,175,55,0.45)) to mark the user's active decade. v1 §3.5 sealed five card surfaces (cardstock, quiet, tinted-metal/wood/fire/earth/water) — none are gold. The hue exists as --gold for tier chips only.

Proposal: add cardstock-active to v1 §3.5 — same fill, gold-25 border, used for "current period" affordances (decade pillar, this-year forecast bar). Or: confirm tier-gold may double as period-gold and codify the dual semantic.
D14.2 — Section-veil gradient (lock C) is not in v2
v2 §9 sealed the card-veil (3px blur + 78% silk overlay). The section-veil pattern in §10C — vertical gradient fading the lower 65% of a list to silk — is new. It's the right tool for "show 3 of 8, fade the rest" surfaces (Life Chapters, multi-pillar).

Proposal: v2.1 adds §9.C section-veil as a third lock primitive. Gradient stops + height ratio token sealed at that point.
D14.3 — Polarity dot palette under week ribbon
§8 week ribbon uses 5 colours under each day cell (catalyst / resistance / neutral / companion / quiet). All five are existing tokens, but the combination as a polarity legend is new. Risk: a designer reads --metalDeep as "metal element" not "companion role."

Proposal: document the polarity-dot legend explicitly in v1 §2.5 (alongside element pigments). Or: derive a separate 5-dot polarity ladder in fresh hues — would require new colour tokens.
D14.4 — Friends V2 four-axis labels overload "Catalyst"
Energy Map's pair card uses Catalyst/Resistance for the user's own chart. Friends V2 reuses Catalyst/Resistance + Companion/Contrast for the relationship between two charts. The same token name now means two different things depending on surface.

Proposal: rename Friends-side labels — e.g. "Lift / Drag" or "Spring / Brake" — to keep one-token-one-meaning. Or accept the overload and add a docstring on each surface clarifying the context.
D14.5 — DM hero downscale from 84 → 54 has no precedent
v1 sealed the archetype blade glyph at 84px (DES_04 §11). Energy Map needs a scaled-down variant (54px) to share vertical space with the identity ribbon. Currently the proportions of the inner star path scale linearly — needs visual confirmation that strokes still read at 54.

Proposal: v1 §6 adds a "compact" archetype variant at 54px with a slightly thicker stroke (0.8 → 1.0) to compensate for the smaller area. Or: keep 84 and reflow Energy Map.
D14.6 — Modal D invite-sheet's monospace link row
Modal D (§11) shows a shareable invite link in JetBrains Mono on a parchment-tinted surface. Monospace strings are codified in v1 §3 only as spec annotations, never as user-readable content.

Proposal: v1 §3 adds a "data string" type role (mono on parchment) for links, redemption codes, and other functional copy that must be visually distinct from prose. Low risk — just naming an existing pairing.
