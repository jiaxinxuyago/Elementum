Translation logic — painterly → silhouette

How each painterly motif reduced to its E3 mark

Apply the same logic when translating stem motifs to silhouettes: extract the single most-iconic shape, drop atmospheric detail, render as one closed silhouette with optional evenodd cutouts. Stems should silhouette-rhyme with their parent element — same vocabulary of curves, weights, and asymmetries.

Metal — blade brushwork reduced to a faceted shard silhouette with internal cutouts. Sharp angular edges, ingot weight at the base, slim tongue rising. Yang stems within Metal (庚 axe / sword) should inherit this faceted-blade weight; Yin (辛 jade hairpin) should inherit only the precision, not the mass.
→ silhouette
Wood — bamboo culm with two leaf clusters becomes a vertical stem with two asymmetric leaf silhouettes meeting at mid-height. Yang stems (甲 great tree) should inherit the vertical structural weight; Yin (乙 vine) should inherit the leaf curl and lateral motion, drop the trunk.
→ silhouette
Fire — flame tongue rising reduces to a three-tongue asymmetric silhouette with an inner cutout. Yang stems (丙 sun) should radiate larger and outward, possibly as a disc with rays; Yin (丁 candle/ember) should focus inward — one small tongue on a base, contained.
→ silhouette
Earth — mountain mass + horizon line becomes layered peaks above a stable base. Yang stems (戊 mountain) should inherit the peak mass and base; Yin (己 cultivated soil) should drop the peaks for horizontal furrows or a small mound — earth as worked surface, not skyline.
→ silhouette
Water — river crests reduce to two horizontal flowing waves. Yang stems (壬 ocean/river) should inherit the cresting scale, possibly with depth/current; Yin (癸 dew/mist) should drop the waves for small droplets or vapor strokes — water at the smallest readable scale.
→ silhouette
